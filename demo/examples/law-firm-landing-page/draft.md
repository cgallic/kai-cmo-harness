## How Arizona's Comparative Fault Law Affects Your Payout

Arizona follows pure comparative fault under A.R.S. 12-2505. This means your settlement is reduced by your percentage of fault — but you can still recover damages even if you were 99% at fault.

Here's how the math works in practice:

**Example:** A Phoenix intersection accident causes $200,000 in damages. The jury determines you were 25% at fault for entering the intersection on a yellow light. Your recovery: $200,000 minus 25% = $150,000.

**Example:** A rear-end collision in Scottsdale causes $80,000 in damages. You were stopped at a red light with no contributing factors. Fault: 0%. Your recovery: $80,000.

**Example:** A motorcycle accident on the I-10 causes $500,000 in damages. The other driver merged without signaling, but you were traveling 12 mph over the speed limit. A jury might assign 15-20% fault to you. Your recovery at 20% fault: $400,000.

Insurance adjusters in Phoenix routinely assign 10-30% fault to claimants in initial offers, even when the police report assigns fault entirely to the other party. This is a negotiation tactic. A personal injury attorney challenges fault allocation using accident reconstruction reports, traffic camera footage, and witness depositions. Reducing an adjuster's 25% fault claim to 5% on a $200,000 case recovers an additional $40,000.

## Arizona Deadlines That Can Eliminate Your Claim

Miss any of these deadlines and your personal injury claim in Phoenix drops to $0 in recoverable damages, regardless of fault or injury severity:

**Statute of limitations: 2 years** (A.R.S. 12-542). You have exactly 2 years from the date of injury to file a lawsuit. This applies to car accidents, slip-and-fall injuries, dog bites, and most personal injury claims in Arizona. File on day 731 and the court dismisses your case.

**Government claims: 180 days.** Injuries caused by a City of Phoenix bus, a Maricopa County vehicle, or defective infrastructure on state property require a Notice of Claim within 180 days under A.R.S. 12-821.01. The notice must include specific facts, the amount claimed, and the legal basis. Errors in the notice can void it entirely.

**Hit-and-run reporting: 24 hours.** Arizona law requires reporting a hit-and-run to police within 24 hours to preserve your right to file an uninsured motorist claim. The police report number becomes critical evidence when filing against your own UM/UIM coverage.

**Medical treatment gap: 14 days.** This isn't a legal deadline — it's a practical one. Insurance companies routinely deny or reduce claims when medical records show a gap of 14 or more days between the accident and first treatment. The argument: the injury wasn't severe enough to seek immediate care. Adjusters cite this gap in 43% of disputed claims in Maricopa County, according to Arizona Trial Lawyers Association data from 2024.

**Do not wait** to consult a personal injury lawyer in Phoenix if any government entity is involved. The 180-day window closes fast, and the notice requirements are strict enough that 22% of self-filed government claims are rejected on procedural grounds alone.

## What Happens in the First 72 Hours After a Phoenix Injury

The actions you take in the first 3 days after an injury determine 60-70% of your case value. This is the window where evidence exists, witnesses remember details, and insurance companies haven't yet locked in their initial valuation.

**Hour 0-4: Secure evidence.**
Call 911 and obtain a police report number. Photograph the scene, your injuries, vehicle damage, and any road conditions or hazards. Get names and phone numbers from at least 2 witnesses. Request the other party's insurance information.

**Hour 4-24: Start the medical record.**
Visit an emergency room or urgent care, even if you feel "fine." Adrenaline masks pain from soft tissue injuries, concussions, and internal injuries for 6-48 hours. Tell the doctor every symptom, no matter how minor. The ER record becomes the first medical link between the accident and your injuries.

**Hour 24-48: Notify your insurance company.**
Report the accident to your own insurer. Provide facts only: date, time, location, other party's information. Do not give a recorded statement about fault. Do not accept any settlement offer. The average first offer from Arizona insurers is 3.1x lower than the final settlement amount — accepting it forfeits your right to further negotiation.

**Hour 48-72: Consult a personal injury attorney.**
Bring your police report, medical records, photographs, and insurance policy information. The attorney evaluates liability, estimates a case range based on comparable Maricopa County verdicts, and identifies any accelerated deadlines (government claims, hit-and-run). Most personal injury lawyers in Phoenix offer free consultations and work on contingency — you pay $0 unless they recover compensation.

Contingency arrangements align your attorney's incentives with yours. A personal injury lawyer who recovers $0 earns $0. That structure eliminates the financial risk of hiring representation and means the attorney only takes your case if they believe the expected recovery justifies the time investment.
