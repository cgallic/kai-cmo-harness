# Landing Page CRO Framework

> **Use when**: Designing landing pages, optimizing conversion rates, running A/B tests on pages, building lead gen or sales pages, diagnosing why a page isn't converting.

---

## Above-the-Fold Formula

Everything above the fold (visible without scrolling) must answer three questions within 5 seconds:
1. **What is this?** (Headline)
2. **Why should I care?** (Subheadline + value proposition)
3. **What do I do next?** (CTA)

### The five above-the-fold elements

| Element | Purpose | Optimization Rule |
|---------|---------|------------------|
| **Headline** | Stop attention, state the core promise | < 70 characters. Specific > clever. Include the outcome, not the process. |
| **Subheadline** | Expand on the headline with proof or specificity | 1-2 sentences. Address the "how" or "why believe this." |
| **Hero image/video** | Show the product, result, or emotional state | Product in use > product alone. Faces looking toward CTA increase conversion. |
| **Primary CTA** | Tell the visitor exactly what to do | Action verb + value outcome. "Get My Free Audit" > "Submit." |
| **Trust signal** | Remove initial skepticism | Logo bar, star rating, or one-line proof ("Trusted by 10,000+ companies"). |

### Above-the-fold template

```
[Logo]                                          [Nav (minimal or hidden)]

# [Headline: The core promise in < 70 chars]
## [Subheadline: How it works or proof it works, 1-2 sentences]

[Primary CTA Button]          [Hero Image/Video/Product Screenshot]

[Trust bar: "Trusted by [logos] | 4.9/5 stars | 10,000+ customers"]
```

### Headline formulas that convert

| Formula | Example | When to Use |
|---------|---------|------------|
| **[Outcome] Without [Pain]** | "Grow Revenue Without Hiring More Salespeople" | When the audience has a known pain point |
| **[Specific Number] [Audience] [Outcome]** | "12,000 Marketers Use This to Cut CPA by 40%" | When you have social proof numbers |
| **[Action Verb] Your [Metric]** | "Double Your Pipeline in 90 Days" | When the promise is measurable |
| **The [Category] That [Differentiator]** | "The CRM That Actually Closes Deals" | When reframing the category |
| **Stop [Pain]. Start [Outcome].** | "Stop Guessing. Start Knowing." | When the pain is emotional |

## Social Proof Placement Hierarchy

Social proof is the single most important trust-building element on a landing page. The type and placement matter.

### Hierarchy of proof strength (strongest to weakest)

| Rank | Proof Type | Impact | Where to Place |
|------|-----------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | **Specific metrics** | Highest | Above fold + near CTA ("$2.4M generated for clients in 2025") |
| 2 | **Named case studies** | Very high | Dedicated section with logo, quote, and result number |
| 3 | **Video testimonials** | High | Mid-page, after benefits section |
| 4 | **Written testimonials with photo + title** | Medium-high | Near objection-handling sections |
| 5 | **Logo bar** | Medium | Above fold (even without testimonials, logos build instant credibility) |
| 6 | **Star ratings / review counts** | Medium | Above fold or near CTA ("4.8/5 from 2,300 reviews on G2") |
| 7 | **User counts** | Low-medium | Above fold ("Join 50,000+ marketers") |
| 8 | **Certifications / badges** | Low | Footer or trust section |
| 9 | **Media mentions** | Low | "As seen in" section (mid or bottom page) |

### Social proof rules

1. **Specificity beats quantity** — "Helped Company X increase revenue by 147% in 6 months" beats "Thousands of happy customers."
2. **Match proof to audience** — If targeting enterprise, show enterprise logos. If targeting startups, show startup logos. Mixed signals reduce trust.
3. **Place proof near friction** — Put testimonials next to the form, near pricing, and beside the CTA. That's where doubt lives.
4. **Use pull quotes** — Extract the most specific, results-oriented sentence from a testimonial and display it in large text. The full testimonial can be expandable.
5. **Refresh quarterly** — Stale testimonials from 3 years ago undermine credibility.

## CTA Hierarchy

Every landing page should have one primary CTA, and optionally secondary and tertiary CTAs for visitors who aren't ready to convert.

### CTA types

| Level | Purpose | Design | Example |
|-------|---------|--------|---------|
| **Primary** | Main conversion action | Large button, high contrast color, above fold + repeated at bottom | "Start Free Trial" / "Get My Audit" |
| **Secondary** | Lower-commitment alternative | Smaller button or text link, below primary | "Watch Demo" / "See Pricing" |
| **Tertiary** | Catch-all for cold visitors | Text link, footer or exit intent | "Read Case Studies" / "Download Guide" |

### CTA copy rules

1. **First person possessive** — "Get My Free Report" converts 24% higher than "Get Your Free Report" (ContentVerve study).
2. **Action + Value** — Verb + what they get. "Start Saving" > "Submit." "Get the Playbook" > "Download."
3. **One primary CTA per page** — Multiple equal CTAs cause decision paralysis. One action, repeated.
4. **Button color** — High contrast against the page background. The specific color matters less than the contrast.
5. **Surround CTA with proof** — Place a micro-testimonial, guarantee, or trust badge directly below the CTA button.

### CTA placement

- **Above the fold** — Always. This is the first opportunity.
- **After benefits section** — Reader has absorbed the value proposition.
- **After social proof** — Reader's objections have been addressed.
- **Bottom of page** — Final opportunity for scroll-through visitors.
- **Sticky/floating CTA** — On mobile, a sticky CTA at the bottom of the screen increases conversions by 10-20%.

## Form Optimization

Forms are where conversion dies. Every unnecessary field reduces completion rate.

### Field reduction data

| # of Fields | Relative Conversion Rate | Notes |
|------------|------------------------|-------|
| 1 (email only) | 100% (baseline) | Highest conversion, lowest lead quality |
| 2 (email + name) | 85-90% | Minimal friction |
| 3 | 75-80% | Sweet spot for most lead gen |
| 4-5 | 60-70% | Acceptable for mid-funnel |
| 6-8 | 40-50% | Significant drop — justify each field |
| 9+ | 25-35% | Only for high-intent bottom-funnel |

### Progressive profiling

Instead of asking everything upfront, collect information over time:

- **First form** (top of funnel): Email only, or email + first name.
- **Second interaction** (content download, webinar registration): Add company name, role.
- **Third interaction** (demo request, free trial): Full qualification fields (company size, budget, timeline).

This approach increases initial conversion by 30-50% while still collecting full data over the customer journey.

### Form design best practices

1. **Single column** — Multi-column forms confuse scanning order.
2. **Labels above fields** — Not inline/placeholder text (disappears on click, causes confusion).
3. **Smart defaults** — Pre-fill country, detect company from email domain.
4. **Inline validation** — Show errors as the user types, not after submission.
5. **Social login** — "Sign up with Google" reduces friction dramatically for B2C.
6. **Privacy line** — "We'll never share your info" next to the submit button. Small text, big impact.
7. **No CAPTCHAs** if possible — Use honeypot fields instead. CAPTCHAs reduce conversion 3-10%.

## Page Speed Impact on Conversion

Page speed is a direct conversion factor. Data from multiple studies:

| Load Time | Conversion Impact |
|-----------|------------------|
| 0-2 seconds | Optimal — no impact |
| 2-3 seconds | -7% conversion rate |
| 3-5 seconds | -20% conversion rate |
| 5-8 seconds | -35% conversion rate |
| 8+ seconds | -50%+ conversion rate |

### Speed optimization checklist

```
[ ] Images compressed (WebP format, lazy loading below fold)
[ ] CSS and JS minified and bundled
[ ] CDN enabled for static assets
[ ] Server response time < 200ms (TTFB)
[ ] No render-blocking resources above fold
[ ] Font loading optimized (font-display: swap)
[ ] Third-party scripts loaded async (analytics, chat widgets)
[ ] Core Web Vitals passing (LCP < 2.5s, FID < 100ms, CLS < 0.1)
[ ] Mobile page speed tested (Google PageSpeed Insights > 80)
```

## Mobile-First Design

70-80% of landing page traffic is mobile. Design for mobile first, then adapt to desktop.

### Mobile-specific rules

1. **Touch targets** — CTA buttons minimum 48x48px with 8px spacing between tap targets.
2. **Font size** — Body text minimum 16px. Headlines minimum 24px. Anything smaller is unreadable.
3. **Single column** — No side-by-side layouts on mobile. Stack everything vertically.
4. **Thumb-zone CTA** — Place the primary CTA in the bottom half of the screen where thumbs naturally rest.
5. **Collapse long sections** — Use accordions for FAQs and feature lists. Don't force endless scrolling.
6. **Sticky CTA** — Float the primary CTA at the bottom of the screen. Visible at all times.
7. **Tap-to-call** — For lead gen pages, a phone number that's tap-to-call on mobile converts well.
8. **Test on real devices** — Emulators miss real-world issues (notch overlap, keyboard push, browser chrome).

## Trust Signals

Trust signals reduce perceived risk at every stage of the page.

### Trust signal placement map

| Page Section | Trust Signal | Purpose |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Above fold** | Logo bar, star rating, user count | Instant credibility |
| **Benefits section** | "Used by [Company Name] to achieve [Result]" | Proof that benefits are real |
| **Near form** | "No credit card required" / "Cancel anytime" | Remove commitment fear |
| **Near pricing** | Money-back guarantee, free trial terms | Remove financial risk |
| **Near CTA** | Security badges, privacy statement | Remove data concern |
| **Footer** | Certifications, compliance badges, contact info | Legitimacy signals |

### Trust signals by industry

| Industry | Most Effective Trust Signals |
|----------|------------------------------|
| **SaaS** | G2/Capterra badges, security certifications (SOC2, ISO), uptime SLA |
| **E-commerce** | Return policy, payment security badges, shipping guarantee |
| **Financial services** | Regulatory licenses, insurance coverage, security certifications |
| **Healthcare** | HIPAA compliance, professional licenses, medical board certifications |
| **Professional services** | Client logos, case studies with numbers, team credentials |

## Pricing Page Optimization

Pricing pages are the highest-intent pages on most sites. Optimization here has outsized impact.

### Pricing page best practices

1. **Three tiers** — The center/recommended tier should be the one you want most people to choose. Highlight it visually.
2. **Anchor high** — Show the enterprise/premium tier first (left to right) to anchor the price. The middle tier looks like a bargain by comparison.
3. **Name tiers by outcome** — "Starter / Growth / Scale" is more compelling than "Basic / Pro / Enterprise."
4. **Feature comparison table** — Below the pricing cards, show a full feature matrix. Scrollable table with checkmarks.
5. **Annual vs monthly toggle** — Default to annual with the savings highlighted ("Save 20%").
6. **Per-unit clarity** — "per user/month" must be immediately clear. Hidden costs destroy trust.
7. **FAQ below pricing** — Address objections ("Can I switch plans?", "What if I need more than X?", "Is there a contract?").
8. **CTA per tier** — Each tier gets its own CTA. Don't force everyone through the same button.
9. **Social proof on pricing** — "Most popular" badge, "Chosen by X companies", testimonial from a customer on the recommended tier.

## FAQ Section

FAQs serve two purposes: objection handling and SEO (FAQ schema can generate rich results).

### FAQ best practices

1. **Address real objections** — Pull from sales call recordings, support tickets, and chatbot transcripts. What do people actually ask?
2. **5-7 questions optimal** — Enough to address major concerns, not so many that the page becomes overwhelming.
3. **Structure answers for scanning** — Bold the key phrase, keep answers to 2-3 sentences.
4. **Use accordion/expandable format** — Shows all questions at once without overwhelming with text.
5. **Place below the pricing section** — This is where objections peak.
6. **Include Schema markup** — `FAQPage` schema for potential rich results in search.

### FAQ question categories

| Category | Example Questions |
|----------|------------------|
| **Pricing** | "Is there a free trial?", "Can I cancel anytime?", "Are there hidden fees?" |
| **Product** | "How long does setup take?", "Does it integrate with X?", "What's the learning curve?" |
| **Support** | "What kind of support do you offer?", "What's your response time?" |
| **Security** | "Where is my data stored?", "Are you SOC2 compliant?" |
| **Comparison** | "How are you different from [Competitor]?", "Do I need to switch from X?" |

## Exit Intent Strategy

Exit intent popups capture 2-8% of abandoning visitors when executed well.

### Exit intent best practices

1. **Offer something valuable** — Discount, free resource, demo booking. Not just "Wait! Don't leave!"
2. **One per session** — Never show the exit intent popup more than once per visit.
3. **Match the page intent** — On a pricing page, offer a discount or free trial extension. On a blog, offer a content upgrade.
4. **Mobile alternative** — Exit intent (mouse movement) doesn't work on mobile. Use scroll-depth trigger (50-70% scroll) or time-on-page trigger (30+ seconds) instead.
5. **A/B test the offer** — Discount vs free resource vs human touchpoint (book a call). Different audiences respond differently.
6. **Suppress for returning visitors** — If someone has already seen the popup or already converted, don't show it again.

## Heatmap Analysis

Heatmaps reveal where visitors actually look, click, and scroll. Use them to diagnose and optimize.

### Types of heatmaps

| Type | What It Shows | What to Look For |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Click map** | Where visitors click | Clicks on non-clickable elements (= design confusion), ignored CTAs, unexpected click clusters |
| **Scroll map** | How far visitors scroll | Drop-off points (most important content must be above the main drop-off), section-by-section attention |
| **Move/attention map** | Where the mouse (proxy for eyes) hovers | Content that draws attention vs content that's ignored |
| **Session recordings** | Individual user journeys | Rage clicks, form abandonment, confusion patterns |

### Heatmap diagnostic framework

| Heatmap Finding | Diagnosis | Fix |
|----------------|-----------|-----|
| CTA gets few clicks | CTA not compelling, or placed below scroll drop-off | Improve copy, move above fold, increase contrast |
| Clicks on non-clickable elements | Users expect interactivity | Make element clickable or redesign to look non-interactive |
| 80% drop off before pricing | Content before pricing isn't compelling | Shorten or restructure above-pricing content |
| Form started but not completed | Form too long or confusing | Reduce fields, add progress bar, fix validation |
| Hero image gets more attention than headline | Image is distracting from the message | Simplify image or use it to direct attention toward headline |

## A/B Testing Prioritization: ICE Framework

ICE (Impact, Confidence, Ease) helps prioritize which tests to run first.

### Scoring

Score each potential test 1-10 on three dimensions:

| Dimension | Question | Score |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| **Impact** | If this test wins, how much will it improve conversion rate? | 1 (marginal) - 10 (transformative) |
| **Confidence** | How confident am I that this test will produce a winner? | 1 (wild guess) - 10 (strong data/precedent) |
| **Ease** | How easy is this test to implement? | 1 (major engineering) - 10 (text change) |

**ICE Score** = (Impact + Confidence + Ease) / 3

### ICE prioritization examples

| Test Idea | Impact | Confidence | Ease | ICE | Priority |
|-----------|--------|------------|------|-----|----------|
| Change headline to include specific number | 8 | 7 | 10 | 8.3 | Test first |
| Add video testimonial above fold | 7 | 6 | 5 | 6.0 | Test second |
| Redesign entire page layout | 9 | 4 | 2 | 5.0 | Test later |
| Move CTA button color from blue to orange | 3 | 3 | 10 | 5.3 | Low priority |
| Add live chat widget | 6 | 5 | 7 | 6.0 | Test second |
| Reduce form from 6 fields to 3 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8.0 | Test first |

### Testing cadence

1. Run 1-2 tests at a time (more creates noise).
2. Each test runs minimum 2 weeks or until 200+ conversions per variant.
3. Document every test result — wins AND losses.
4. Re-test big winners 6 months later (audience and context change).

## Tactical Checklist

Before launching any landing page:

```
[ ] Above-the-fold contains: headline, subheadline, CTA, hero visual, trust signal
[ ] Headline is < 70 characters and states a specific outcome
[ ] Single primary CTA repeated 3-4 times on the page
[ ] Social proof present (logos, testimonials, metrics)
[ ] Form fields minimized (ask only what you need at this funnel stage)
[ ] Page loads in < 3 seconds on mobile
[ ] Mobile design tested on real device
[ ] All tracking installed (GA4, Meta pixel, LinkedIn Insight, Google Ads)
[ ] UTM parameters configured for all traffic sources
[ ] Thank you / confirmation page exists with next step
[ ] FAQ section addresses top 5-7 objections
[ ] A/B test hypothesis documented and test running
[ ] Heatmap tool installed (Hotjar, Microsoft Clarity)
[ ] Exit intent configured (desktop + mobile alternative)
```
