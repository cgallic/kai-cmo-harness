# LinkedIn AI Content Detection and Feed Checklist

> **Use when:** Reviewing LinkedIn posts/articles before handoff or publish approval, especially research-backed thought leadership.

Source packet: `knowledge/research/linkedin-ai-systems/`.

---

## Feed Fit

- [ ] The piece is anchored to one clear professional topic/entity cluster.
- [ ] The first 200 characters name the problem, mechanism, or contrarian correction.
- [ ] The post includes at least one concrete mechanism, metric, paper, patent, system name, or workflow.
- [ ] The reader gets a save-worthy artifact: checklist, teardown, rule, table, diagram, or repeatable process.
- [ ] The CTA asks for a meaningful response, not shallow engagement bait.
- [ ] External links are moved to the first comment unless source citation is more important than reach.

## Anti-Slop / AI Detection QA

- [ ] No generic AI phrases: delve, tapestry, unlock, game-changer, leverage, in conclusion, it is important to note.
- [ ] No repeated contrastive construction pattern: "It's not X, it's Y" more than once.
- [ ] Paragraph lengths vary naturally.
- [ ] At least one sentence reflects human judgment: what changed, what surprised us, what we would do differently.
- [ ] The draft avoids fake certainty about hidden ranking weights.
- [ ] If AI systems are discussed, claims are tied to primary research, patents, engineering posts, or clearly labeled third-party studies.

## Originality Signals

- [ ] The core claim is not a recycled LinkedIn trope.
- [ ] The draft names the false/common narrative and corrects it with evidence.
- [ ] It includes operator implications for founders, marketers, sales teams, or AI builders.
- [ ] It includes a specific example instead of broad advice.
- [ ] The ending gives a decision rule, not a generic motivational wrap-up.

## Confidence Gate

- [ ] High-confidence claims come from `linkedin_ai_cross_verification.md` or primary sources.
- [ ] Medium-confidence claims are phrased as estimates/findings from a named study.
- [ ] Low-confidence or black-box claims are omitted unless explicitly framed as hypotheses.

## Pass Criteria

A LinkedIn draft passes this checklist only if it is:

1. semantically clear enough for retrieval,
2. specific enough to create dwell/saves,
3. human enough to avoid AI-slop suppression,
4. honest enough to survive source review.
