# Multi-Location Business Audit Checklist

> **Use when:** Auditing a business with 2-500+ physical locations that must balance centralized brand consistency with decentralized local execution. Best fit for franchises, clinic networks, dental groups, restaurant chains, retail chains, multi-office law firms, med spas, fitness studios, home service franchises, and any business scaling across geographies.

---

## Per-Location Google Business Profile (GBP)

> **Why it matters:** Each location needs its own verified GBP to appear in local search and maps for its service area. A single brand-level profile cannot capture local demand. Inconsistent or unmanaged location profiles leak leads to competitors in every market.

- [ ] Every location has its own claimed and verified GBP
- [ ] Business name is consistent across all locations (no keyword stuffing, no unauthorized name variations)
- [ ] Primary category is identical across all locations unless service mix genuinely differs
- [ ] Secondary categories are consistent where services overlap
- [ ] NAP (name, address, phone) is correct and unique per location
- [ ] Each location's phone number routes to that specific location (not a shared national line)
- [ ] Business hours are accurate per location, including holiday hours
- [ ] Location-specific photos uploaded: storefront, interior, team, work samples (minimum 10 per location)
- [ ] New photos added per location on a regular cadence (monthly minimum)
- [ ] Google Posts published per location (weekly target, can be centrally created and localized)
- [ ] Q&A section seeded with location-relevant questions and answers
- [ ] Services list populated per location, reflecting any local variation
- [ ] Location-specific attributes set (accessibility, payment methods, amenities, etc.)
- [ ] Website link points to that location's dedicated landing page (not the homepage or store locator)
- [ ] UTM parameters appended to each location's GBP website link for attribution
- [ ] Review response happening per location (see Review Management section)
- [ ] GBP audit cadence established: quarterly review of all location profiles for accuracy
- [ ] Bulk management tool in use for organizations with 10+ locations (GBP bulk upload, third-party platform)

## Local Pages at Scale

> **Why it matters:** A store locator alone does not rank. Each location needs its own indexable, content-rich landing page to capture "[service] near [city]" and "[brand] [city]" queries. Template pages with the city name swapped out get filtered as thin content.

- [ ] Individual landing page exists for every location on the main website
- [ ] Each location page has a unique URL structure (e.g., `/locations/city-name/` or `/city-name/`)
- [ ] Location pages contain unique, substantive content (not a template with city name swapped)
- [ ] Each page includes the location's specific address, phone number, and hours
- [ ] Location-specific testimonials or reviews displayed on the page
- [ ] Local team photos and staff bios included per location where feasible
- [ ] Location-specific service offerings highlighted if they differ by market
- [ ] Embedded Google Map showing the specific location
- [ ] Driving directions or neighborhood/landmark references for local relevance
- [ ] LocalBusiness schema markup (JSON-LD) implemented per location page
- [ ] Schema includes correct NAP, geo-coordinates, opening hours, and parent organization
- [ ] Internal linking connects each location page to relevant service pages
- [ ] Internal linking connects nearby location pages to each other where relevant
- [ ] Location pages link to or display location-specific promotions or events
- [ ] Each location page passes Core Web Vitals on mobile
- [ ] Location pages are included in the XML sitemap
- [ ] Canonical tags are correct (no cross-location duplication issues)
- [ ] Store locator exists as a hub page linking to all individual location pages

## Centralized Brand + Decentralized Execution

> **Why it matters:** Multi-location businesses fail when the brand fragments across markets or when local managers are blocked from executing because they lack tools and guidelines. The system must make it easy to stay on-brand while empowering local speed.

- [ ] Brand guidelines documented and accessible to all location managers (voice, tone, logo usage, colors, photography style)
- [ ] Approved creative asset library available (logos, templates, stock photos, ad templates)
- [ ] Template system exists for local managers to create on-brand content (Canva brand kit, templated social posts, email templates)
- [ ] Centralized approval workflow defined for locally produced marketing (who approves, response SLA, escalation path)
- [ ] Social media policy documented for location managers (what they can post, what requires approval, prohibited content)
- [ ] Co-op advertising fund structure defined (contribution model, eligible spend categories, reimbursement process)
- [ ] Local marketing playbook distributed to every location manager (what to do in their first 90 days, ongoing monthly tasks)
- [ ] Brand audit cadence established (quarterly or semi-annual review of all location marketing for brand consistency)
- [ ] New location onboarding checklist includes all marketing setup steps
- [ ] Communication channel exists between corporate marketing and location managers (Slack, Teams, intranet)
- [ ] Location managers trained on brand guidelines and marketing tools
- [ ] Process exists to collect and share best practices across locations

## Review Management at Scale

> **Why it matters:** Reviews are the strongest trust signal in local buying decisions and directly influence GBP visibility. At scale, unmanaged reviews in any single location drag down brand perception and suppress that location's search performance.

- [ ] Centralized review monitoring dashboard in use (Birdeye, Podium, ReviewTrackers, or equivalent)
- [ ] Dashboard aggregates reviews across all locations and all platforms (Google, Yelp, Facebook, industry-specific)
- [ ] Per-location review response SLA defined and enforced (target: within 24 hours, max 48 hours)
- [ ] Response templates available that allow personalization (not identical copy-paste across locations)
- [ ] Decision framework defined: which reviews does corporate handle vs. location manager
- [ ] Review generation process standardized across all locations (post-service text/email with direct review link)
- [ ] Per-location review score tracked over time (rating, count, velocity)
- [ ] Location performance benchmarking includes review metrics (location vs. location comparison)
- [ ] Escalation process documented for negative reviews (1-2 star) including response timeline and resolution steps
- [ ] Critical review alerts configured (e.g., any 1-star review triggers immediate notification to regional manager)
- [ ] Review data feeds into location performance scoring and operational decisions
- [ ] Review insights aggregated quarterly to identify systemic issues across locations
- [ ] Strong reviews repurposed as testimonials on location pages and marketing assets

## Location-Level Reporting

> **Why it matters:** Aggregate reporting hides underperformers. Per-location visibility is the only way to allocate budget intelligently, identify which locations need help, and prove ROI at the unit level.

- [ ] Per-location traffic tracking configured (Google Analytics segments, property per location, or equivalent)
- [ ] Per-location conversion tracking in place (form submissions, calls, bookings attributed to each location)
- [ ] Per-location ad spend and ROI tracked separately
- [ ] Per-location call volume and lead quality measured (call tracking numbers per location)
- [ ] GBP Insights reviewed per location on a regular cadence (monthly minimum)
- [ ] Per-location keyword ranking tracking for local terms
- [ ] Centralized dashboard aggregates all location data into a single view
- [ ] Dashboard supports drill-down from portfolio level to individual location
- [ ] Location-vs-location performance benchmarking established (traffic, leads, conversion rate, cost per lead, reviews)
- [ ] Underperforming locations flagged automatically based on defined thresholds
- [ ] Monthly or quarterly location performance report distributed to stakeholders
- [ ] Attribution model accounts for cross-channel local journeys (GBP -> website -> call)

## Local Paid Media

> **Why it matters:** Paid media must be geo-targeted to each location's service area to avoid budget waste. Shared campaigns without location segmentation blend strong and weak markets, making optimization impossible.

- [ ] Per-location Google Ads campaigns or ad groups with location-specific targeting (or shared campaigns with location extensions and geo-targeting)
- [ ] Location extensions active on all Google Ads campaigns
- [ ] Per-location Local Services Ads (LSA) set up where the business category is eligible
- [ ] Geo-targeted Meta/Facebook ads configured per location's service area
- [ ] Budget allocation model defined (equal split, performance-based, population-based, or hybrid)
- [ ] Budget reallocation cadence established (monthly or quarterly based on per-location ROI)
- [ ] Call tracking numbers assigned per location for paid media attribution
- [ ] Ad creative includes location-specific elements (city name, local imagery, location-specific offers)
- [ ] Negative location targeting prevents overlap between adjacent location campaigns
- [ ] Landing pages for paid traffic are location-specific (not homepage or generic page)
- [ ] Paid media performance reviewed per location weekly or biweekly
- [ ] Co-op fund utilization tracked against eligible paid media spend

## KaiCalls per Location

> **Why it matters:** Every missed call at any location is a lost lead. Multi-location businesses need consistent call handling across all locations while capturing location-level data for performance management.

- [ ] KaiCalls AI receptionist (`kaicalls.com`) configured for each location
- [ ] Each location's KaiCalls instance trained on location-specific data: services offered, business hours, team members, physical address, and service area
- [ ] Location-specific FAQs and common questions loaded into each instance
- [ ] Centralized notification routing configured with location tagging (every lead tagged to its originating location)
- [ ] Call handling experience is consistent across all locations (same greeting structure, qualification flow, and professionalism)
- [ ] After-hours and overflow call handling active at every location
- [ ] Per-location call analytics accessible from a centralized dashboard
- [ ] Lead capture data flows into CRM or lead management system with location attribution
- [ ] Scheduling integration configured per location (connecting to each location's calendar or booking system)
- [ ] Test calls completed at every location to verify conversation quality and data accuracy
- [ ] Spanish language support (or other relevant languages) configured where the market requires it
- [ ] New location onboarding includes KaiCalls setup as a standard step

## Local SEO at Scale

> **Why it matters:** Citation consistency across dozens or hundreds of locations is a compounding advantage. Inconsistent NAP data, missing directory listings, or weak local link profiles suppress visibility in every affected market.

- [ ] NAP data is identical across all directories for every location
- [ ] Citation management tool in use for all locations (Yext, Moz Local, BrightLocal, or equivalent)
- [ ] Core directories submitted per location: Google, Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places
- [ ] Industry-specific directories submitted per location where relevant
- [ ] Duplicate listings identified and suppressed across all locations
- [ ] Per-location LocalBusiness schema markup implemented with correct `branchOf` or `parentOrganization` relationships
- [ ] Organization-level schema connects the brand entity to all location entities
- [ ] Per-location backlink building strategy in place (local sponsorships, chamber of commerce, community partnerships)
- [ ] Local keyword targeting defined per market (accounting for regional terminology and competition differences)
- [ ] Per-location content strategy addresses locally relevant topics (local events, community involvement, market-specific services)
- [ ] Hreflang or geo-targeting configured if locations span multiple countries or languages
- [ ] Local link acquisition tracked per location and reported centrally
- [ ] NAP audit conducted quarterly across all locations and directories

## Social Media per Location

> **Why it matters:** The social media model must match the business's scale and staffing. Per-location accounts create local relevance but require local management. A single brand account is easier to control but risks losing local connection. The wrong choice creates either brand fragmentation or local irrelevance.

- [ ] Strategic decision documented: single brand account, per-location accounts, or hybrid model
- [ ] If per-location accounts: consistent branding (profile photos, cover images, bio format) enforced
- [ ] If per-location accounts: local content mix defined (local events, team spotlights, location-specific offers, community content)
- [ ] If per-location accounts: local team trained on posting guidelines and response protocols
- [ ] If per-location accounts: content calendar template provided to maintain posting consistency
- [ ] If centralized account: location tagging used on relevant posts
- [ ] If centralized account: local content interspersed with brand content (not 100% corporate messaging)
- [ ] If centralized account: community management process handles location-specific comments and questions
- [ ] Location-specific promotions or events surfaced on social regardless of account model
- [ ] User-generated content from customers encouraged and reshared with location attribution
- [ ] Social media performance tracked per location where possible (even on centralized accounts via post-level tagging)
- [ ] Crisis communication protocol documented for location-specific incidents on social media

## Operational Consistency

> **Why it matters:** Marketing drives demand to the door. If the in-location experience varies wildly, reviews suffer, retention drops, and marketing spend is wasted. Operational consistency is a marketing multiplier.

- [ ] Mystery shopping or quality audit program in place (quarterly minimum per location)
- [ ] Customer experience standards documented and measurable
- [ ] Customer experience consistency measured across locations (NPS, CSAT, or equivalent by location)
- [ ] Staff training standardized with consistent onboarding materials
- [ ] Technology stack consistent across locations (POS, CRM, booking system, phone system)
- [ ] New location launch marketing playbook documented (GBP setup, local pages, citations, paid media, KaiCalls, review generation kickoff)
- [ ] New location launch timeline defined with marketing milestones
- [ ] Location closure or rebrand process documented (redirect URLs, update citations, transfer reviews where possible, update schema)
- [ ] Consistent service/product offerings with documented local exceptions
- [ ] Customer feedback loop exists from operations back to marketing (e.g., common objections heard on calls inform ad copy)

## Exclusions

This checklist does **not** cover:

- Industry-specific regulatory compliance (healthcare HIPAA, legal ethics, financial disclosures, etc.)
- Single-location businesses (use `local-service-business-checklist.md` instead)
- E-commerce or DTC business models without physical locations
- International multi-location considerations beyond basic hreflang/geo-targeting
- Franchise legal agreements or FDD requirements
- Real estate or property management (distinct business model)
