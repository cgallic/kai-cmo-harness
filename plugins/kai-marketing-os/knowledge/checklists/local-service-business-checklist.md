# Local Service Business Audit Checklist

> **Use when:** Auditing a business that serves a geographic area and depends on local demand, phone calls, reviews, and Google visibility. Best fit for solo operators and small teams such as plumbers, cleaners, lawyers, HVAC, landscapers, roofers, electricians, painters, dentists, chiropractors, and contractors.

---

## Google Business Profile (GBP)

> **Why it matters:** For local service businesses, GBP is often the highest-leverage marketing asset. It drives map visibility, trust, and phone calls faster than most website work.

- [ ] Profile claimed and verified
- [ ] Business name matches the real-world business name exactly (no keyword stuffing)
- [ ] Primary category is correct and as specific as possible
- [ ] Secondary categories added where relevant (up to 9)
- [ ] NAP (name, address, phone) matches GBP, website, and directories exactly
- [ ] Business hours are accurate, including holiday hours
- [ ] Service area is defined correctly for service-area businesses
- [ ] Services list is populated with clear descriptions
- [ ] Business description is written naturally and avoids spam
- [ ] Photo set includes logo, cover photo, team, work samples, and location/context where relevant
- [ ] At least 10 quality photos uploaded
- [ ] New photos added regularly (monthly minimum)
- [ ] Google Posts published regularly (weekly target)
- [ ] Q&A section includes seeded common questions and answers
- [ ] Messaging enabled only if the business can respond quickly
- [ ] "Request a Quote" / booking button configured correctly
- [ ] Products/services catalog populated where applicable
- [ ] Relevant attributes set (ownership, accessibility, service options, etc.)
- [ ] Website link points to the right page (homepage or location page)
- [ ] GBP website link includes UTM parameters for tracking

---

## Reviews & Reputation

> **Why it matters:** Reviews are the strongest trust signal in local buying decisions. More high-quality reviews usually means more calls, better close rates, and stronger GBP/LSA performance.

- [ ] Total review count is competitive for the market
- [ ] Average rating is healthy (target 4.5+)
- [ ] Review velocity is steady, not bursty
- [ ] Every review gets a response within 48 hours
- [ ] Negative reviews receive calm, professional, offline-resolution responses
- [ ] Review generation process exists after every completed job
- [ ] Primary review request method is text with a direct review link
- [ ] Google review link is generated and easy to send
- [ ] Secondary review platforms claimed where relevant (Yelp, Facebook, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Nextdoor, etc.)
- [ ] No fake reviews or review gating practices
- [ ] Customers are encouraged to leave photo reviews when appropriate
- [ ] Strong reviews are reused as testimonials across website and marketing assets

---

## KaiCalls AI Receptionist

> **Why it matters:** Every missed inbound call is a likely lost job. Local service demand decays fast because callers contact multiple providers and book whoever answers first.

- [ ] KaiCalls (`kaicalls.com`) configured on the main business number
- [ ] AI agent trained on services, pricing ranges, service areas, and FAQs
- [ ] Lead-qualification questions configured for the business model
- [ ] Call notifications route to owner/operator via SMS and/or email
- [ ] Scheduling tool integration configured (Jobber, Housecall Pro, Calendly, etc.)
- [ ] After-hours calls answered by KaiCalls instead of voicemail
- [ ] Overflow handling enabled for unanswered daytime calls
- [ ] Test call completed and reviewed for conversation quality and lead capture accuracy
- [ ] Spanish language support configured if the market requires it

---

## Local SEO & Citations

> **Why it matters:** Citation consistency and localized relevance improve trust with Google and support visibility beyond GBP itself.

- [ ] NAP is identical across all major listings
- [ ] Citation audit completed with a tool such as Moz Local or BrightLocal
- [ ] Core directories submitted: Yelp
- [ ] Core directories submitted: Facebook Business Page
- [ ] Core directories submitted: Apple Maps / Apple Business Connect
- [ ] Core directories submitted: Bing Places
- [ ] Core directories submitted: Nextdoor Business
- [ ] Core directories submitted: BBB
- [ ] Relevant industry directories submitted (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Houzz, Avvo, Thumbtack, etc.)
- [ ] Website includes service area pages or location pages for key cities served
- [ ] Service area pages use unique content, not city-name swaps
- [ ] LocalBusiness schema markup implemented (JSON-LD)
- [ ] Service schema markup added on service pages where relevant
- [ ] Contact/location page includes an embedded Google Map where appropriate
- [ ] Title tags and H1s target local intent naturally ("[Service] in [City]")
- [ ] City and neighborhood references appear naturally in body copy

---

## Google Local Services Ads (LSA)

> **Why it matters:** LSA is the highest-intent paid placement for many service categories. It sits above standard search ads and charges per lead rather than per click.

- [ ] LSA eligibility confirmed for the business category and geography
- [ ] Google Screened / Google Guaranteed requirements completed where available
- [ ] Starting budget set based on expected lead flow and close rate
- [ ] Service types selected tightly around desired jobs
- [ ] Service areas defined realistically to protect lead quality
- [ ] Business hours aligned with actual response coverage
- [ ] Lead review and dispute process exists for invalid leads within 30 days
- [ ] Callback speed target is under 5 minutes for new LSA leads
- [ ] KaiCalls is configured to answer LSA calls so no paid lead hits voicemail
- [ ] Reviews are actively maintained because they influence LSA visibility
- [ ] Weekly checks cover lead quality, CPL, close rate, and dispute rate

---

## Website Essentials (Local Business)

> **Why it matters:** The website is usually a support asset for GBP, reviews, and paid local channels. It still has to convert mobile visitors and reinforce trust fast.

- [ ] Phone number appears in the header on every page
- [ ] Phone number is clickable with a `tel:` link
- [ ] Phone CTA is visually prominent on desktop and mobile
- [ ] Contact form exists but is secondary to phone for high-intent visitors
- [ ] Form notifications route to email and SMS or another fast-response channel
- [ ] Dedicated service pages exist for each core service
- [ ] Service area/location pages exist for key geographies served
- [ ] About page includes owner story, photo, licenses, insurance, and years in business
- [ ] Before/after gallery or project portfolio exists
- [ ] Trust signals are visible: licensed, bonded, insured, certifications, BBB, etc.
- [ ] Site is mobile-first and works cleanly on common phone sizes
- [ ] Mobile page speed is under 3 seconds where feasible
- [ ] Site uses HTTPS
- [ ] Google Analytics and Search Console are installed

---

## Social Media (Local Business)

> **Why it matters:** For local services, social media is mostly a trust layer. The goal is proof of work, local visibility, and familiarity, not broad-reach brand storytelling.

- [ ] Facebook Business Page is fully set up and active
- [ ] Nextdoor Business profile is claimed
- [ ] Instagram is active if the work is visual
- [ ] Before/after photos are posted regularly (minimum 2x/week target)
- [ ] Job completion posts highlight the service and location served
- [ ] Seasonal tips and maintenance content are published periodically
- [ ] The business engages in local community conversations where relevant
- [ ] Social posts avoid constant hard-sell messaging
- [ ] GBP posts are maintained and can reuse strong social content

---

## Offline & Referral Marketing

> **Why it matters:** A large share of local service revenue comes from referrals, repeat work, and neighborhood visibility. Ignoring offline touchpoints leaves easy demand on the table.

- [ ] Yard signs or job-site signs are used with customer permission
- [ ] Vehicle branding exists (wrap or magnetic signage)
- [ ] Door hangers or neighbor outreach happen after completed jobs where appropriate
- [ ] Business cards are available and consistently used
- [ ] Referral incentive exists and is easy to explain
- [ ] Post-job follow-up includes thank-you, review ask, and referral ask
- [ ] Repeat-customer tracking exists for recurring or seasonal services
- [ ] Local sponsorships or community placements are evaluated
- [ ] Partnerships with complementary local businesses exist or are being developed

---

## Job Management & Operations

> **Why it matters:** Marketing performance collapses when lead handling, booking, invoicing, and follow-up are weak. Operations directly affect close rate, review volume, and repeat revenue.

- [ ] Job management software is in use (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, etc.)
- [ ] Online booking or scheduling is available where appropriate
- [ ] Appointment reminders are automated, preferably by text
- [ ] Invoices are sent promptly and professionally
- [ ] On-site or fast digital card payment is available
- [ ] Follow-up sequence is standardized: completed job -> thank you -> review request -> referral ask
- [ ] Customer database is maintained outside of ad hoc phone contacts
- [ ] Seasonal or repeat-service reminders are automated where applicable

## Exclusions

This checklist does **not** cover:

- [ ] Industry-specific compliance rules (legal, medical, financial, etc.)
- [ ] Multi-location or franchise business models
- [ ] E-commerce-heavy business models
- [ ] National or remote-first service businesses
