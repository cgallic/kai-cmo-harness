# Fictional selection examples

These are illustrative business models, with no customer records or observed results. Do not inherit their targets or permissions. None implies authorized execution.

## Repair service

Select `services-trades` from an authorized service catalog and job process. The conversion unit could be a completed, paid job, with cancellation and refund rules. Reconcile job/payment records before calling a baseline zero. Calls and forms are leading interactions. P3/P10 precede a P18 experiment; choose a receptionist only if actual channel evidence justifies one.

## Product shop

Select `ecommerce` from a product catalog and fulfilled-order process. Configure eligible fulfilled units or net orders explicitly. Product units, purchasers and revenue differ; distinguish cash, store credit and returns. Gift buyers may differ from end users. A checkout demo is not live payment or fulfillment evidence.

## Software with consulting

Select `saas` and `b2b-services` with separate evidence and confidence. Map software to activated, retained paying accounts and consulting to delivered, paid engagements. Keep two offer goals, baselines and journeys. Shared messaging may be useful; adding subscriptions to consulting engagements as one conversion metric is not.

## Booking and practice models

Use `retail-hospitality` for completed visits/stays, `rentals-events` for fulfilled rentals/events, and `professional-practices` for permitted completed engagements. Capacity, cancellations, confidentiality and who books versus pays change the plan. Names alone do not establish fit.

## Unresolved profile

Use no selected profiles when evidence is insufficient. R0 records what is known and asks about the value exchange, purchaser and meaningful outcome. Defer channel recommendations that depend on a profile or missing permissions. A capability listed in a repository does not establish account access.
