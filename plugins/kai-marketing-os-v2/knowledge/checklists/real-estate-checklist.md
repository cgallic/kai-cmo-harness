# Real Estate Marketing Audit Checklist

> **Use when:** Auditing a real estate agent, team, brokerage, property manager, or real estate investor whose marketing depends on personal brand, local expertise, and lead generation. Best fit for individual agents, small teams, and boutique brokerages where every listing won and every buyer captured is a direct revenue event.

---

## Portal & Listing Presence

> **Why it matters:** Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, and Homes.com are where buyers start their search. An incomplete or unoptimized agent profile on these portals means losing deals to competitors who show up with better photos, more reviews, and a complete sales history. The listing itself is the ad — bad photos and boilerplate descriptions kill interest before a showing ever happens.

- [ ] Zillow profile claimed with professional headshot, full bio, past sales data, and active reviews
- [ ] Realtor.com agent profile claimed and fully completed
- [ ] Redfin agent profile claimed and optimized
- [ ] Homes.com agent profile created and current
- [ ] Trulia profile synced (shares Zillow data but verify independently)
- [ ] All active listings syndicated to every major portal (Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, Homes.com, Trulia, MLS feeds)
- [ ] Listing photos are professional quality — shot by a real estate photographer, not a phone camera
- [ ] Photo count per listing meets or exceeds market standard (25+ photos for most markets)
- [ ] Listing descriptions are compelling, buyer-focused narratives — not MLS boilerplate or agent jargon
- [ ] Listing descriptions highlight lifestyle and neighborhood context, not just square footage and room counts
- [ ] Virtual tours or 3D walkthroughs (Matterport or equivalent) are provided for active listings
- [ ] Video walkthroughs are produced for listings above $500K (or local luxury threshold)
- [ ] Drone/aerial photography is used for properties with notable lots, views, or locations

## Google Business Profile & Local SEO

> **Why it matters:** When someone searches "[city] real estate agent" or "[neighborhood] homes for sale," Google decides who shows up. A verified, active GBP with strong reviews and consistent local signals is the difference between being found and being invisible. Neighborhood-level content builds the topical authority that wins both traditional search and AI Overviews.

- [ ] Google Business Profile claimed and verified (category: Real Estate Agent, Real Estate Agency, or appropriate variant)
- [ ] NAP (name, address, phone) is consistent across GBP, website, Zillow, Realtor.com, and all directories
- [ ] Google review count is competitive for the market (target: 50+ reviews, 4.8+ stars)
- [ ] "[City] real estate agent" and "[neighborhood] homes for sale" keywords are actively targeted on the website
- [ ] Dedicated neighborhood pages exist on the website — one per farm area — with unique, substantive content
- [ ] Market reports and local stats pages are published and updated monthly or quarterly
- [ ] Blog content targets specific buyer and seller questions ("How much are homes in [neighborhood]?", "Is [city] a good place to buy?")
- [ ] RealEstateAgent schema markup is implemented on the website (JSON-LD)
- [ ] Google Posts are published weekly: just listed, just sold, market updates, open houses, tips
- [ ] GBP photos include professional headshot, team photos, listing highlights, and neighborhood/community context
- [ ] GBP business description clearly states service area, specialization, and what makes the agent different
- [ ] GBP Q&A section includes seeded common questions and answers (commission, process, areas served)

## Lead Generation System

> **Why it matters:** Real estate is a lead generation business. An agent without a systematic way to capture, qualify, and route leads is relying on luck. Every channel — website, ads, open houses, portals — must feed into a single system that turns interest into appointments.

- [ ] IDX website is live with property search functionality (kvCORE, Sierra Interactive, Luxury Presence, or similar)
- [ ] Lead capture exists on property searches — registration wall, soft-gate, or "save search" prompt
- [ ] Landing pages exist for specific campaigns: buyer guide, free home valuation, neighborhood guide, relocation guide
- [ ] Facebook and Instagram lead ads are running with proper targeting (location, demographics, interests, lookalike audiences)
- [ ] Google Ads campaigns target high-intent terms: "[city] homes for sale", "[neighborhood] real estate", "homes for sale near [landmark]"
- [ ] KaiCalls AI receptionist (`kaicalls.com`) is configured on the main business line — agents miss calls during showings, closings, and client meetings, and every missed buyer or seller call decays within minutes
- [ ] KaiCalls is trained on service areas, current listings, showing scheduling, and common buyer/seller questions
- [ ] After-hours and overflow calls route to KaiCalls instead of voicemail
- [ ] Zillow Premier Agent or Realtor.com paid lead programs are evaluated (running if ROI-positive for the market)
- [ ] Open house lead capture is systematic — digital sign-in sheet feeding directly into CRM, not paper forms that sit in a drawer
- [ ] Referral system with past clients is active and structured — not just hoping people remember you
- [ ] Lead source tracking exists so cost per lead and conversion rate are known by channel

## CRM & Follow-Up

> **Why it matters:** Speed-to-lead is the single most predictive metric for real estate conversion. The first agent to respond wins the client more often than the best agent. A CRM without automated follow-up sequences is just an expensive address book.

- [ ] CRM is in active use (Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, LionDesk, Sierra Interactive, Wise Agent, or equivalent)
- [ ] Every lead is entered into the CRM and tagged by source (Zillow, Google Ads, open house, referral, sign call, etc.)
- [ ] Speed-to-lead target: first contact within 5 minutes of inquiry (critical — Harvard/MIT study shows 100x drop-off after 5 minutes)
- [ ] Automated drip campaign exists for new buyer leads (property alerts, market updates, value-add content)
- [ ] Automated drip campaign exists for new seller leads (market reports, pricing strategy, prep tips)
- [ ] Past client nurture campaign is running (annual check-ins, market updates, home anniversary)
- [ ] Sphere of influence drip is active (monthly value-add content to non-client contacts)
- [ ] Long-term nurture sequence exists for not-yet-ready leads (6-18 month timeline buyers/sellers)
- [ ] Transaction anniversary follow-ups are automated (purchase anniversary, home-iversary cards or emails)
- [ ] Market update emails go to the full database at least monthly
- [ ] CRM integrates with email, phone, and text so all communication is logged in one place
- [ ] Lead status pipeline is defined and maintained (new, contacted, qualified, appointment set, active client, closed, nurture)

## Social Media & Content

> **Why it matters:** Real estate is a visual, personality-driven business. Social media is where agents build recognition, demonstrate local expertise, and stay top-of-mind between transactions. Video-first content gets algorithmic preference on every platform. An agent who posts once a week is invisible; an agent who posts daily with a content mix is the one people remember when they need to buy or sell.

- [ ] Instagram account is active and optimized (bio, link in bio, highlights for listings/testimonials/neighborhoods)
- [ ] Facebook business page is active with regular posting and community engagement
- [ ] TikTok account is active with property tours, market updates, and agent personality content
- [ ] YouTube channel exists for long-form property tours, neighborhood guides, and market reports
- [ ] Posting cadence: minimum 5x/week across platforms combined
- [ ] Content mix is balanced: just listed (20%), just sold (20%), market insights (20%), personal/lifestyle (20%), educational/tips (20%)
- [ ] Neighborhood and community content is published regularly — local events, restaurant spotlights, school info, lifestyle
- [ ] Video-first strategy is in place — Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts get algorithmic preference across all platforms
- [ ] Stories are used daily on Instagram and Facebook for casual, behind-the-scenes, day-in-the-life content
- [ ] Hashtag strategy includes local and real estate-specific tags
- [ ] Engagement is two-way: commenting on local business posts, responding to DMs, participating in community groups
- [ ] Content is created in batches (weekly content days) for consistency, not scrambled ad hoc

## Neighborhood Farming

> **Why it matters:** The most consistent top producers work a defined geographic farm. Farming builds name recognition, positions the agent as the neighborhood expert, and generates listing leads over time. Agents who "work everywhere" become known nowhere.

- [ ] Farm area(s) are defined — specific neighborhoods or subdivisions, not an entire city or zip code
- [ ] Direct mail goes to the farm on a regular cadence (monthly minimum): just listed/just sold postcards, market reports, seasonal mailers
- [ ] Door knocking or drop-by strategy exists for the farm (new listings, sold announcements, seasonal drop-offs)
- [ ] Neighborhood event sponsorship is active or planned (block parties, school events, sports teams, holiday events)
- [ ] Hyperlocal content about the farm area is published on the website and social media
- [ ] Community Facebook group participation is active for farm neighborhoods
- [ ] Nextdoor presence is established and maintained in farm neighborhoods
- [ ] Farm area market data is tracked and reported back to the neighborhood (monthly or quarterly farm report)

## Sphere of Influence (SOI) Marketing

> **Why it matters:** The National Association of Realtors reports that 40% of buyers use an agent referred by a friend or family member, and 25% use the same agent they used before. SOI is the highest-converting, lowest-cost lead source in real estate — but only when it is systematically maintained, not left to chance.

- [ ] SOI list is maintained and categorized in the CRM (past clients, friends/family, professional contacts, vendors, neighbors)
- [ ] Monthly touchpoint to the full SOI exists (not just a holiday card in December)
- [ ] Pop-by gifts or drop-offs happen quarterly (pie at Thanksgiving, pumpkins in October, calendars in January, etc.)
- [ ] Client appreciation event is held annually (summer BBQ, movie night, holiday party, family photo day)
- [ ] Birthday and home anniversary tracking is active with automated or personal outreach
- [ ] Referral asks are systematic — built into the post-close process and periodic SOI touchpoints, not random one-offs
- [ ] SOI members receive genuine value content (not just "thinking of buying or selling?" emails)
- [ ] SOI is actively grown — new contacts added from every open house, community event, and transaction

## Listing Marketing

> **Why it matters:** Every listing is a marketing asset twice: it markets the property to buyers and it markets the agent to future sellers. Agents who treat listings as billboards for their brand — with professional media, strategic pre-marketing, and post-sale follow-up — generate their next listing from the current one.

- [ ] Professional photography is non-negotiable for every listing, regardless of price point
- [ ] Professional videography and drone photography are used for listings at $500K+ (or local luxury threshold)
- [ ] Staging consultation is offered or virtual staging is provided for vacant properties
- [ ] Pre-listing social media teaser campaign runs before the listing hits MLS ("Coming Soon" posts, countdown, sneak peek)
- [ ] "Coming Soon" strategy is executed before MLS entry where market and MLS rules allow
- [ ] Property-specific landing page or single-property website exists for listings above the median price
- [ ] Just-listed campaign fires on day one: social media posts across all platforms, email blast to database, door hangers or mailers to neighbors
- [ ] Open house marketing includes a Facebook event, boosted post, Nextdoor post, and neighborhood outreach
- [ ] Just-sold campaign runs after every closing: social proof post ("SOLD in X days for $X over asking") paired with a "thinking of selling?" call to action
- [ ] Seller receives a marketing report showing all activity and exposure their listing received

## Reviews & Testimonials

> **Why it matters:** In real estate, a stranger trusts a review more than a sales pitch. Strong reviews on Google and the portals are the trust layer that converts a lead's curiosity into a consultation request. Video testimonials are the highest-converting content type in the business.

- [ ] Google reviews are the primary focus (drives GBP visibility and general trust)
- [ ] Zillow reviews are actively solicited (buyers search here first)
- [ ] Realtor.com reviews are maintained
- [ ] Video testimonials from happy clients are collected (even 30-second selfie testimonials on closing day)
- [ ] Testimonials on the website are organized by buyer and seller experience
- [ ] Review request process is built into the closing workflow: ask at closing + follow up 1 week later with a direct review link
- [ ] Case study format is used for notable transactions (first-time buyer in a competitive market, above-ask sale, investment property win)
- [ ] Negative reviews receive calm, professional responses with an offline resolution offer
- [ ] Best reviews and testimonials are repurposed across social media, listing presentations, and marketing materials

## Personal Brand & Differentiation

> **Why it matters:** There are roughly 1.5 million active real estate agents in the US. Most of them look, sound, and market exactly the same. The agents who win consistently have a clear positioning that makes them the obvious choice for a specific type of client, transaction, or neighborhood. Without differentiation, the only remaining lever is price — and discounting commissions is a race to zero.

- [ ] Clear positioning exists: luxury agent, first-time buyer specialist, investor-focused, neighborhood expert, relocation specialist, downsizer expert, new construction, or similar niche
- [ ] Professional headshot is current (updated within the last 2 years)
- [ ] Consistent visual brand is applied across all platforms: colors, fonts, logo, photo style
- [ ] Personal website exists — not just a page on the brokerage site
- [ ] "About" page tells a story: why real estate, community involvement, credentials, personality — not a resume
- [ ] Media appearances or local press features are pursued and displayed
- [ ] Professional designations are earned and displayed where relevant (GRI, CRS, ABR, SRS, CLHMS, SRES, e-PRO, etc.)
- [ ] Elevator pitch is rehearsed and specific — not "I help people buy and sell homes"
- [ ] Brand is consistent from first Google impression through closing: the agent a prospect finds online is the same agent they meet in person

**Maximum weighted score:** 175

**Normalized score:** (weighted total / 175) x 100 = score out of /100

**Grading:**
- **A:** 90+ — Top-producer marketing operation. Maintain momentum and optimize for market shifts.
- **B:** 75-89 — Strong foundation with clear growth opportunities. Prioritize the weakest sections.
- **C:** 60-74 — Functional but leaving deals on the table. Significant upside available with focused effort.
- **D:** 40-59 — Major gaps across multiple areas. Structured improvement plan needed before the next market shift exposes them.
- **F:** <40 — Marketing infrastructure is missing or broken. Start with GBP, reviews, and a CRM before anything else.

## Exclusions

This checklist does **not** cover:

- [ ] Brokerage-level operations (recruiting, agent training, office management)
- [ ] Multi-state or national franchise marketing coordination
- [ ] Property management marketing (tenant acquisition, owner acquisition)
- [ ] Commercial real estate marketing (CRE has a fundamentally different buyer journey)
- [ ] Real estate investing deal sourcing (wholesaling, direct mail to distressed, skip tracing)
- [ ] MLS compliance rules (vary by market — consult your local MLS)
- [ ] State-specific real estate advertising regulations (varies by state — consult your broker or attorney)
- [ ] Team or brokerage website platform selection
