# Restaurant & Food / Beverage Business Audit Checklist

> **Use when:** Auditing a restaurant, cafe, bar, food truck, bakery, catering company, or ghost kitchen. Best fit for food and beverage businesses where the product is edible, margins are thin, competition is hyper-local, and visual marketing drives demand. Covers dine-in, takeout, delivery, and event catering models.

---

## Google Business Profile (GBP)

> **Why it matters:** GBP is the single highest-leverage marketing asset for restaurants. "Restaurants near me" is one of the most common local searches, and the map pack drives foot traffic, phone calls, and online orders directly.

- [ ] Profile claimed and verified
- [ ] Business name matches the real-world name exactly (no keyword stuffing)
- [ ] Primary category is correct and specific (e.g., "Italian Restaurant" not just "Restaurant")
- [ ] Secondary categories added where relevant (bar, pizza delivery, catering, etc.)
- [ ] NAP (name, address, phone) matches GBP, website, and all third-party platforms exactly
- [ ] Business hours are accurate, including weekday vs. weekend differences
- [ ] Special hours set for holidays, seasonal closures, and private event days
- [ ] Menu link is live and points to a mobile-friendly page (not a PDF)
- [ ] Reservation link is configured (OpenTable, Resy, or direct booking page)
- [ ] Order-online link is configured (direct ordering preferred over third-party)
- [ ] Food-specific attributes set: dine-in, takeout, delivery, outdoor seating, alcohol served, wheelchair accessible
- [ ] High-quality food photography uploaded (minimum 20 photos covering dishes, interior, exterior, drinks, team)
- [ ] New photos added at least monthly (seasonal dishes, specials, events)
- [ ] Google Posts published weekly: specials, events, new menu items, seasonal offerings
- [ ] Q&A section seeded with common questions: parking, dress code, allergy accommodations, group dining capacity, private events, corkage policy
- [ ] Every review receives a response within 24 hours
- [ ] Messaging enabled only if staff can respond within 15 minutes during business hours
- [ ] Website link includes UTM parameters for tracking

## Delivery & Ordering Platforms

> **Why it matters:** Delivery platforms are a reality for most restaurants. They bring volume but take 15-30% commission. The goal is to be visible on platforms while building a direct ordering channel that protects margins.

- [ ] Present on relevant platforms: DoorDash, UberEats, Grubhub, Postmates (market-dependent)
- [ ] Menu is accurate and up-to-date on every platform (synced within 24 hours of any change)
- [ ] Photos on all menu items across all platforms (items with photos get 30%+ more orders)
- [ ] Item descriptions are clear and appetite-driving (not just ingredient lists)
- [ ] Pricing strategy defined: markup to offset platform fees, or absorb fees to stay competitive
- [ ] Modifier and add-on options configured correctly (extra sauce, substitutions, sides)
- [ ] Out-of-stock items are marked unavailable promptly (not left up to disappoint customers)
- [ ] Direct ordering system in place: own website ordering via Toast, Square, ChowNow, or BentoBox
- [ ] Direct ordering is promoted in-store with signage, table tents, and receipt messaging
- [ ] Platform-specific promotions configured during slow periods (free delivery, BOGO, new customer discount)
- [ ] Platform reviews monitored and responded to on every platform
- [ ] Delivery packaging tested for food quality on arrival (temperature, presentation, leak-proof)
- [ ] Ghost kitchen or virtual brand strategy evaluated if applicable

## Food Photography & Visual Content

> **Why it matters:** Food is the most visual product category in marketing. A single great food photo outperforms paragraphs of copy. Restaurants that invest in visual content consistently outperform those that do not on every digital channel.

- [ ] Professional food photography shoot completed for core menu items
- [ ] Lighting is consistent across photos (natural light or controlled studio lighting, never mixed)
- [ ] Styling is consistent: same plate styles, backgrounds, and color palette across the menu
- [ ] Hero shots exist for signature dishes (the 5-10 items that define the restaurant)
- [ ] Seasonal menu items photographed before each seasonal launch
- [ ] Drink/cocktail photography exists if bar program is part of the business
- [ ] User-generated content (UGC) strategy in place: encourage diners to photograph and tag
- [ ] Instagram-worthy plating and presentation considered as part of menu development
- [ ] Behind-the-scenes content produced: kitchen prep, chef at work, ingredient sourcing, farmer relationships
- [ ] Short-form video content produced: plating process, cooking techniques, tableside preparations, atmosphere walkthroughs
- [ ] Interior and exterior photos updated seasonally (patio setup in spring, holiday decor in winter)
- [ ] Photo library organized and accessible for use across all marketing channels

## Reviews & Reputation

> **Why it matters:** A restaurant with 200 reviews at 4.4 stars will consistently beat a restaurant with 30 reviews at 4.8 stars in local search and consumer trust. Volume, velocity, and response rate all matter.

- [ ] Google review count is competitive for the local market (target: 100+ reviews minimum, 300+ in competitive metros)
- [ ] Google rating is healthy (target: 4.3+ stars in competitive markets, 4.5+ in smaller markets)
- [ ] Yelp profile claimed and actively managed
- [ ] TripAdvisor profile claimed and managed (mandatory for tourist-area restaurants)
- [ ] Every review receives a response within 24 hours on every platform
- [ ] Positive review responses are personalized (not copy-paste templates)
- [ ] Negative review handling follows the formula: empathize, acknowledge specifics, take offline, invite back
- [ ] Review generation process is active: table cards with QR code, receipt prompts, follow-up text for online/delivery orders
- [ ] Photo reviews are encouraged (ask guests to share their meal shots in their review)
- [ ] Review velocity is steady week over week (not bursty from one-time campaigns)
- [ ] Best reviews are repurposed as testimonials on the website and social media
- [ ] Staff is trained to never argue publicly with a reviewer

## Social Media

> **Why it matters:** Instagram is essentially mandatory for restaurants. Food is inherently visual and shareable. Social media drives discovery, creates FOMO, and keeps the restaurant top of mind between visits.

- [ ] Instagram account is active and on-brand (this is the primary social platform for restaurants)
- [ ] TikTok account is active: behind-the-scenes, cooking videos, food prep ASMR, staff personality content
- [ ] Facebook page is maintained: events, community engagement, older demographic reach
- [ ] Posting cadence: minimum 4 posts per week across platforms
- [ ] Daily specials posted to Instagram Stories (with location tag and relevant hashtags)
- [ ] User-generated content reshared with credit (builds community and provides free content)
- [ ] Engagement with local food bloggers and micro-influencers (invite for complimentary tastings, not just cold DMs)
- [ ] Seasonal content calendar planned: holiday menus, patio season launch, new menu rollouts, restaurant anniversary
- [ ] Hashtag strategy includes local tags (#[city]eats, #[neighborhood]food) and food-specific tags (#[cuisine]food, #foodporn)
- [ ] Reel and TikTok content shows the food being made, plated, or enjoyed (not just static photos)
- [ ] Instagram bio includes a Linktree or equivalent with links to menu, reservations, ordering, and events
- [ ] Social profiles are cross-linked from the website, GBP, and email signatures

## Menu Engineering & Pricing

> **Why it matters:** The menu is both a product and a marketing tool. Menu design, item placement, descriptions, and pricing psychology directly impact average check size, food cost percentage, and margin per guest.

- [ ] Menu layout uses the golden triangle (top-right, top-center, center are high-attention zones)
- [ ] High-margin items are visually highlighted (boxes, icons, bold text, "chef's favorite" callouts)
- [ ] Anchor pricing strategy is in place (one premium item makes everything else look reasonable)
- [ ] Menu descriptions sell the experience, not just ingredients ("slow-braised for 8 hours" vs. "braised beef")
- [ ] Sensory language is used in descriptions (crispy, creamy, hand-pulled, wood-fired, house-made)
- [ ] Seasonal or limited-time offers are always on the menu to create urgency and repeat visits
- [ ] Online menu matches the in-house menu exactly (or clearly states differences for delivery-only items)
- [ ] Online menu loads fast, is mobile-optimized, and is NOT a PDF
- [ ] Menu prices are reviewed quarterly against food costs and competitive set
- [ ] Menu items are categorized clearly (not buried in walls of text)
- [ ] Allergen information is accessible (on-menu symbols, separate allergen guide, or staff training)

## Local SEO

> **Why it matters:** When someone searches "[cuisine type] restaurant [city]" or "best [food] near me," local SEO determines whether the restaurant appears. This goes beyond GBP into website content and structured data.

- [ ] Website has a dedicated location page with embedded Google Map
- [ ] NAP is consistent across GBP, website, all delivery platforms, and all directory listings
- [ ] Primary keywords targeted: "[cuisine type] restaurant [city/neighborhood]" (e.g., "Thai restaurant Buckhead")
- [ ] Secondary keywords targeted: "best [dish] in [city]", "[restaurant name] menu", "restaurants near [landmark]"
- [ ] Schema markup implemented: Restaurant, Menu, FoodEstablishment, LocalBusiness (JSON-LD)
- [ ] Menu items have structured data where possible (name, description, price)
- [ ] Listed on local food guides, food blogs, and "best of" roundup articles
- [ ] Actively pursued inclusion in "best [cuisine] in [city]" articles (pitch food bloggers and local press)
- [ ] Website has unique content about the restaurant story, chef background, ingredient sourcing
- [ ] Internal linking connects menu pages, location page, event page, and reservation page
- [ ] Image alt text describes food items specifically ("wood-fired margherita pizza" not "food photo")

## Reservation & Booking System

> **Why it matters:** Friction kills conversions. Every extra step between "I want to eat there" and a confirmed reservation is a potential lost guest. The phone should never go unanswered during peak hours.

- [ ] Online reservation system is live: OpenTable, Resy, Yelp Reservations, or direct through website
- [ ] Reservation widget is embedded on the website (not just a link to an external page)
- [ ] Reservation confirmation sent via text and/or email immediately after booking
- [ ] Reminder sent 2-4 hours before the reservation (reduce no-shows)
- [ ] Private event and catering inquiry form exists on the website
- [ ] Large party booking process is clear (how many guests, deposit requirements, set menu options)
- [ ] KaiCalls AI receptionist (kaicalls.com) configured to handle phone reservations, answer menu/hours questions, and take takeout orders when staff is busy during service
- [ ] Phone rollover to KaiCalls is tested during peak hours to confirm no calls go to voicemail
- [ ] Waitlist system in place for walk-in heavy restaurants (Yelp Waitlist, Waitwhile, or in-house)

## Email & Loyalty

> **Why it matters:** Repeat customers are 5-7x cheaper to convert than new ones. A restaurant that builds an email list and loyalty program turns one-time diners into regulars and regulars into advocates.

- [ ] Email list is actively built: receipt capture, WiFi login page, reservation follow-up, table tent signup
- [ ] Welcome email sent immediately after signup with a first-visit or next-visit incentive
- [ ] Newsletter sent weekly or biweekly: upcoming events, new menu items, seasonal specials, chef stories
- [ ] Loyalty program in place: digital punch card or points system via Toast, Square, or standalone app
- [ ] Birthday and anniversary emails sent with a personalized offer (free dessert, complimentary drink, discount)
- [ ] Re-engagement campaign targets lapsed customers (no visit in 60+ days) with a compelling reason to return
- [ ] Email content includes food photos (not text-only newsletters)
- [ ] Unsubscribe rates are monitored and list hygiene is maintained
- [ ] VIP or "regulars" segment exists for high-frequency guests with exclusive perks or early access to events

---

## Events & Community

> **Why it matters:** Events bring people in on slow nights, create social media buzz, build community ties, and open a high-margin catering revenue stream. They also generate press coverage and backlinks for SEO.

- [ ] Events calendar is published on the website and updated on GBP
- [ ] Regular recurring events are scheduled: trivia night, live music, wine tasting, cooking class, prix fixe dinner, happy hour specials
- [ ] Local partnerships are active: collaborations with breweries, distilleries, farms, neighboring restaurants, local artists
- [ ] Catering services are marketed on the website with a dedicated page, sample menus, and inquiry form
- [ ] Corporate catering is actively pursued: office lunches, meeting catering, corporate event hosting
- [ ] Private dining and buyout options are clearly listed with capacity, pricing structure, and booking process
- [ ] Community sponsorships are evaluated: local sports teams, school events, charity dinners, food bank donations
- [ ] Events are promoted across all channels: email, social media, GBP Posts, in-store signage
- [ ] Post-event content is captured and shared: photos, attendee testimonials, recap posts
- [ ] Seasonal and holiday events are planned at least 6 weeks in advance

## Exclusions

This checklist does **not** cover:

- [ ] Multi-location or franchise restaurant operations
- [ ] Food manufacturing, CPG, or retail grocery businesses
- [ ] Alcohol-only businesses (breweries, distilleries, wine bars) without a food program
- [ ] Food safety, health code compliance, or HACCP requirements
- [ ] Staffing, labor scheduling, or back-of-house operations
- [ ] POS system selection or financial management
