### How GROWTH MODE Helped GeoLinks Reframe Fixed Wireless Credibility and Support a 3x+ Valuation Story

The company had started with a small team of engineers serving unconnected rural residents. It grew into a leading telecommunications provider, earned fastest-growing-company recognition, won major broadband infrastructure opportunities, and built a reputation around closing the U.S. digital divide.

That created a strategic communications challenge: GeoLinks needed more than visibility. It needed the right people to understand why its approach was credible, expandable, and commercially valuable.

#### The Challenge: GeoLinks Had the Proof, But the Category Needed Reframing

GeoLinks was not trying to manufacture a story from nothing.

The company had real momentum. It had grown from rural connectivity roots into a business serving companies, communities, and anchor institutions. Its own About page describes the company as one of the fastest growing companies in America, with a reputation as dependable partners, thought leaders, and innovators.

The problem was that GeoLinks' market did not always know how to evaluate fixed wireless.

In the Broadband Bunch transcript, Skyler described the challenge plainly: fixed wireless had both positive and negative attention in the industry. The negative side came from misconceptions about what the technology could and could not do. He argued that fixed wireless was reliable when engineered properly, and that buyers should hold providers accountable instead of blaming the category.

That distinction mattered.

GeoLinks was not selling a commodity internet connection. It was asking the market to believe in a different infrastructure model: a flexible, lower-footprint, cost-effective way to connect rural schools, hospitals, businesses, and communities that traditional fiber builds often could not reach quickly or economically.

That market education is hard to do with sales collateral alone.

The company needed a visibility system that could make its expertise legible to multiple audiences at once: customers evaluating service reliability, partners evaluating expansion potential, policymakers evaluating broadband deployment, and investors evaluating long-term enterprise value.

#### The Answer: Turn Technical Proof Into Public Credibility

GROWTH MODE's work sat at the intersection of PR, executive authority, and strategic narrative.

The goal was not only to "get press." It was to make GeoLinks' credibility easier for the market to recognize.

That meant building communications around the proof GeoLinks already had:

- Category expertise in fixed wireless and broadband infrastructure.
- A clear founder story, with Skyler as a technical operator and industry voice.
- A mission tied to closing the digital divide.
- Public-sector credibility through broadband awards and policy conversations.
- Business momentum supported by fastest-growing-company recognition.
- A differentiated technology story around ClearFiber and GeoLinks' network footprint.

GROWTH MODE's broader methodology, as described on its agency site, defines PR as more than press. The agency frames PR as how a company cultivates opportunity through strategic and authentic relationship building. For GeoLinks, that meant helping the company show up not only as an internet provider, but as a category leader.

The old perception was: fixed wireless is risky, limited, or unreliable.

The new perception was: properly engineered fixed wireless can be serious infrastructure for rural, suburban, and urban connectivity.

That shift matters because credibility compounds. Once the story is clear, every proof point has somewhere to land. Awards become evidence. Podcast appearances become category education. Funding announcements become validation. Executive visibility becomes a trust asset instead of a vanity exercise.

#### What Other Founders Can Learn

GeoLinks is a useful example for founders, executives, and industry experts because the company did not need public expertise as decoration.

It needed public expertise as translation.

When your category is complex, technical, regulated, or misunderstood, your market may not be able to see the value you are building. That does not mean the value is missing. It means the story is under-translated.

Strategic PR closes that gap.

It gives your expertise a public shape. It turns internal proof into external credibility. It helps the right audiences understand why your work matters before a sales call, investor meeting, partnership conversation, or policy discussion ever begins.

That is the real value of founder visibility. Not becoming louder. Becoming easier to trust.
