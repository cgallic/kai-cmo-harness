**Trigger:** `lead.magnet.day_2` (Loops event)

**Send timing:** 2 days after audit request if no diagnostic call is booked

## Subject Line
Primary: Cheap leads can get expensive
Variant A: The lead cost trap
Variant B: Form fills are not jobs

## Preview Text
The cheapest lead can still be the most expensive one to chase.

## Body
A $12 Facebook lead can be worse than a $90 lead.

That sounds wrong until you check what happens after the form. Did the person answer the phone? Did they live in your service area? Did they want the job you actually sell? Did the team call back within five minutes?

This is where local service campaigns get messy. The ad platform sees a conversion. The owner sees a receptionist chasing names that will never book.

Northstar treats that as the main system to fix.

For a new account, we want the first report to answer four questions:

1. Which ad angle created the lead?
2. Which lead became a real conversation?
3. Which call became a booked estimate?
4. Which service area deserves more spend next week?

When those answers are missing, every budget decision turns into a guess. When they are present, Facebook ads become easier to fund because the owner can see what the next dollar is meant to test.

If your current report stops at cost per lead, the audit score will show where to add call and sales context.

This is also why the follow-up process belongs in the ad conversation. A better ad cannot rescue a lead that waits three hours for a callback. The next test should define both the message and the response rule.
