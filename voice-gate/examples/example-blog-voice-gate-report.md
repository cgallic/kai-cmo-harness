## Detailed Issues

### Issue 1 — "I repeat" / bolded-repeat fires 3× in one post
**Severity:** High
**Category:** Voice-guide violation
**Location:** Lines 58, 100, 112
**Voice-guide rule:** §1.2 "Use the literal phrase 'I repeat' max once per post." Also §2.2 translation rule: "**Bold** the second occurrence. Let the typography do the work."
**Current text (verbatim):**
> Line 58: "**Do not force a fit.** That is one of my repeated rules in PR. **Do not force a fit.**"
> Line 100: "Do not attach files. I repeat. **Do not attach files.**"
> Line 112: "Spell it correctly. I repeat. **Spell it correctly.**"

**Why it's an issue:** The voice guide caps the "I repeat" signature move at 1× per post. The post fires it three times — once with the literal phrase on line 100, once as a paraphrased "one of my repeated rules" bolded-repeat on line 58, and a third literal "I repeat" on line 112. When a signature move fires every ~40 lines the emphasis stops emphasizing and the pattern reads as a crutch rather than a signature.

**Recommended fix:** Pick the single strongest occurrence — the line 112 "Spell it correctly" beat has the pet-peeve "Hi Lexi" payoff and earns its place. Replace the other two with different emphasis patterns from §8 — a short sentence on its own line, a "Period." close, or a blockquote.

### Issue 2 — Sing-songy intro paragraph
**Severity:** High
**Category:** Sing-songy rhythm / cadence monotony
**Location:** Line 21 (opening paragraph)
**Voice-guide rule:** §3 Sentence Architecture ("Mostly short to medium… punchy short closers") + §2.4 cold-open rule + Editorial: triple-list cadence monotony (not explicitly in the voice guide but implicit in §3 and §8).
**Current text (verbatim):**
> A podcast pitch that books the show is short, personalized, timely, and does the host's research for them. It names one specific episode you listened to, answers the question "why you, why now, why this audience" in two or three sentences, and proves you can deliver with a credential, a clip, or a recent result. That is the short version. Now let me show you how that actually plays out, because in 15+ years of PR I have sent thousands of pitches, killed plenty of bad ones, and watched a few sentences land my clients on shows their competitors could not get into.

**Why it's an issue:** Sentence 1 lists four attributes (short / personalized / timely / does-the-research). Sentence 2 re-lists three of them as verbs (names / answers / proves). Sentence 4 ends on another triple-list (sent / killed / watched). Three of the four sentences end on three-item lists. The cadence becomes predictable by sentence two. "That is the short version" is a meta-beat that delays the payload just as the reader is leaning in.

**Recommended fix:** Cut "That is the short version. Now let me show you how that actually plays out." Merge the "15+ years" receipt into sentence 2 as evidence for the definition. Break at least one of the remaining triple-lists into a different sentence shape — a short knockout, a conditional, or a named-client receipt.

### Issue 3 — Four-bullet "You are pitching X, not Y" monotony
**Severity:** High
**Category:** Sing-songy rhythm / cadence monotony
**Location:** Lines 31–34 ("Here is what that means for your pitch")
**Voice-guide rule:** Editorial: identical-shape cadence monotony. §3 Sentence Architecture and §8 Emphasis Patterns both warn against repeated sentence shapes.
**Current text (verbatim):**
> - You are pitching a conversation, not a press release. Skip the announcement energy.
> - You are pitching the host's audience, not the host. Prove you know who listens.
> - You are pitching a format, not a generic slot. Solo expert? Co-host debate? Case study interview? Match the format.
> - You are pitching timing, not just a topic. A host who just released an episode on your exact subject does not need another one next week.

**Why it's an issue:** All four bullets open "You are pitching X, not Y." The rhetorical move is strong once; four in a row becomes a drumbeat the reader tunes out by bullet two. The concept in each bullet is distinct — the delivery flattens them into one mold.

**Recommended fix:** Keep bullet 1 as the anchor pattern. Rewrite bullets 2–4 into different structures — a question, a short imperative, a named-client receipt, a conditional.

## Systemic Patterns Worth Addressing

1. **Triple-list cadence is the dominant rhythm issue.** Sentences ending on three-item lists appear in the intro, the four-bullet anatomy block, the three-pitch example, the anatomy-of-pitch section, the FAQ section, and the recap. A single pass that rewrites every third triple-list into a different sentence shape — a short knockout, a conditional, a named receipt, or a question — would fix ~40% of the flagged cadence issues.
2. **Signature moves are stacking at 3× each.** "I repeat" + bolded-repeat fires 3×, the bolded-emphasis pattern fires 3×, the contrastive "X, not Y" closer fires 8× total. Rule of thumb for the rewrite: each signature move fires at most once per post.
3. **Hedged receipts where named-client receipts would land harder.** "Per my own HARO receipts" (line 100) and "I had a client recently" (line 105) both under-perform compared to the named-partner + percentage receipts the voice guide calibrates against in §12.3. The persona file carries named anecdotes with specifics — swap in one or two on the rewrite.
4. **Algorithmic Authorship pass was not run cleanly.** Voice guide §2.4 requires an AA pass after the voice pass. Line 34 has the condition before the main clause; lines 52–56 are noun-phrases where verb-first would extract better in an AI Overview. A 10-minute AA sweep would catch both.
