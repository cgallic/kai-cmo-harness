# Healthcare / Medical Business Audit Checklist

> **Use when:** Auditing a healthcare provider that markets to patients within a geographic area. Covers dental practices, medical clinics, chiropractic offices, physical therapy practices, med spas, optometrists, dermatologists, urgent care centers, mental health practices, and veterinary clinics. These businesses are subject to HIPAA and medical advertising regulations, which override all other marketing considerations.

---

## HIPAA Compliance & Medical Advertising Rules

> **Why it matters:** A single HIPAA violation can result in fines from $100 to $50,000 per incident (up to $1.5M annually per violation category), criminal prosecution, and practice-ending reputational damage. No amount of marketing excellence matters if compliance fails. **This section is non-negotiable and must be evaluated first.**

- [ ] Patient testimonials have written HIPAA authorization on file before use in any marketing material
- [ ] No before/after photos used without explicit written patient consent (separate from general treatment consent)
- [ ] Before/after photos stored securely with consent documentation linked to each image
- [ ] No guaranteed outcomes in any marketing material ("we treat" not "we will cure"; "may help" not "will fix")
- [ ] All claims about success rates or outcomes are substantiated with verifiable data
- [ ] Advertising complies with state medical board rules for the specific license type (dentist, MD, DO, DC, PT, NP, etc.)
- [ ] Provider credentials displayed accurately — no misleading titles or implied specializations without board certification
- [ ] Business Associate Agreement (BAA) in place with every marketing vendor that could access PHI (email platform, CRM, analytics, call tracking, etc.)
- [ ] Website forms are encrypted (HTTPS + TLS) and submitted data stored in HIPAA-compliant systems
- [ ] Website privacy policy explicitly addresses health information handling
- [ ] Retargeting pixels do not transmit Protected Health Information to ad platforms (Meta Pixel health data issue, Google Analytics PHI exposure)
- [ ] No custom audiences built from patient lists uploaded to ad platforms without a BAA and patient authorization
- [ ] Email marketing tool is HIPAA-compliant if used for patient communication (Paubox, LuxSci, or a platform with a signed BAA)
- [ ] Patient information is never disclosed in review responses — even if the patient shared it first in their review
- [ ] Staff trained on what can and cannot be shared on social media (no patient photos from treatment rooms, no identifiable information)
- [ ] Marketing materials reviewed by a compliance officer or healthcare attorney at least annually
- [ ] SMS/text communication has documented patient opt-in consent
- [ ] Telehealth marketing complies with state licensing requirements (provider must be licensed in the patient's state)

## Google Business Profile (GBP)

> **Why it matters:** For healthcare providers, GBP is the primary discovery channel. Patients searching "[specialty] near me" see the map pack first. An incomplete or inaccurate profile costs appointments every day.

- [ ] Profile claimed and verified for each practice location
- [ ] Business name matches the legal practice name exactly (no keyword stuffing like "Best Dentist Springfield")
- [ ] Primary category is the most specific match (e.g., "Pediatric Dentist" not just "Dentist"; "Dermatologist" not "Doctor")
- [ ] Secondary categories cover additional services (e.g., "Cosmetic Dentist" alongside "Dentist")
- [ ] All services listed individually with descriptions
- [ ] Insurance plans accepted are noted in the business description or services section
- [ ] Telehealth availability indicated if offered
- [ ] Provider photos uploaded — patients want to see who they are visiting before booking
- [ ] Office photos uploaded (waiting room, treatment rooms, exterior) — reduces new-patient anxiety
- [ ] At least 15 quality photos uploaded across categories
- [ ] New photos added monthly (new staff, office updates, seasonal content)
- [ ] Business hours accurate, including walk-in availability and holiday hours
- [ ] After-hours or urgent care availability clearly communicated
- [ ] Google Posts published weekly (health tips, new services, provider introductions, seasonal wellness reminders)
- [ ] Q&A section seeded with common questions: insurance accepted, new patient process, parking/transit, telehealth options, languages spoken, wait times
- [ ] Appointment booking link configured and functional
- [ ] Website link points to the correct page (homepage or location-specific page)
- [ ] GBP link includes UTM parameters for tracking
- [ ] Multiple provider profiles created where GBP supports individual practitioner listings (doctors, dentists, therapists)

## Patient Reviews & Reputation

> **Why it matters:** Healthcare decisions are high-stakes and emotionally charged. Patients rely on reviews more than almost any other purchasing decision. Review volume and rating directly affect whether a patient books or keeps scrolling.

- [ ] Google review count is competitive for the market (target: 50+ reviews minimum, 100+ for competitive markets)
- [ ] Google average rating is 4.5 stars or higher
- [ ] Review velocity is steady (not bursty or suspiciously clustered)
- [ ] Healthgrades profile claimed, complete, and monitored
- [ ] Zocdoc profile active and maintained (if used for patient booking)
- [ ] Vitals.com profile claimed and accurate
- [ ] RateMDs profile claimed and accurate
- [ ] WebMD provider directory listings complete
- [ ] Specialty-specific platforms monitored and maintained:
  - [ ] RealSelf (cosmetic/aesthetic procedures, med spas, dermatology)
  - [ ] Psychology Today (mental health practices, therapists, psychiatrists)
  - [ ] 1-800-Dentist / DentistFinder (dental practices)
  - [ ] AAHA accreditation listing (veterinary clinics)
- [ ] Automated post-appointment review request in place (text message with direct Google review link, sent within 2 hours of visit)
- [ ] Review request workflow avoids HIPAA issues (no mention of treatment type in the request message)
- [ ] Every review receives a response within 48 hours
- [ ] Review responses NEVER reference patient conditions, treatments, or visit details (HIPAA)
- [ ] Negative review response protocol: acknowledge concern, invite offline conversation, do not argue or disclose details
- [ ] Reputation monitoring active for all providers by individual name
- [ ] Strong reviews repurposed as website testimonials (with written consent)

## Patient Acquisition Funnel

> **Why it matters:** Healthcare has high lifetime patient value but also high friction in the booking process. Every unnecessary step between "I need a doctor" and "appointment confirmed" costs the practice revenue. The average medical office misses 30%+ of inbound calls during busy periods.

- [ ] New patient booking flow is clear and available via phone and online
- [ ] Online scheduling available through at least one channel (Zocdoc, website direct booking, EHR patient portal)
- [ ] Online scheduling is prominently placed — not buried in navigation
- [ ] KaiCalls AI receptionist (`kaicalls.com`) configured for the practice phone line to handle appointment requests, insurance questions, and after-hours calls
- [ ] KaiCalls trained on practice services, providers, accepted insurance plans, office hours, and common patient FAQs
- [ ] KaiCalls after-hours mode active (no patient call should ever reach voicemail)
- [ ] KaiCalls overflow enabled for unanswered daytime calls during peak volumes
- [ ] Test call completed and reviewed for conversation quality and scheduling accuracy
- [ ] New patient intake forms available online for download or digital completion before the visit (reduce in-office friction)
- [ ] Insurance verification process communicated clearly on website and during booking
- [ ] Accepted insurance plans listed on the website and updated at least quarterly
- [ ] Self-pay / cash-pay pricing disclosed where appropriate (price transparency regulations vary by state)
- [ ] Telehealth option available, promoted, and easy to book
- [ ] Same-day or next-day appointment availability displayed when available (high-intent conversion driver)
- [ ] New patient welcome flow exists (confirmation email/text, intake forms link, directions, what to bring, what to expect)

## Website & Conversion

> **Why it matters:** The practice website must convert anxious patients who are comparing multiple providers. Trust signals, provider personality, and ease of booking matter more than visual flair.

- [ ] Provider bio pages include photo, credentials, board certifications, specialties, education, and a personal touch (interests, philosophy of care)
- [ ] Each provider bio has a direct "Book with this provider" CTA
- [ ] Condition/service pages include educational content (not just a paragraph — patients are researching)
- [ ] Service pages explain what to expect during the procedure or visit (reduces anxiety, builds trust)
- [ ] Patient education resources section exists (articles, videos, FAQs — establishes authority and improves SEO)
- [ ] Insurance accepted page is complete and current
- [ ] New patient information page covers: what to bring, arrival time, intake process, cancellation policy
- [ ] Phone number appears in the header on every page, is clickable (`tel:` link), and is visually prominent
- [ ] KaiCalls backup ensures calls answered even when front desk is unavailable
- [ ] Contact form exists with clear expectation on response time
- [ ] Mobile-optimized (patients search and book on phones — mobile experience must be seamless)
- [ ] Page load time under 3 seconds on mobile
- [ ] ADA accessible (WCAG 2.1 AA compliant) — healthcare websites face disproportionate ADA lawsuit risk
- [ ] Patient portal link prominently placed for existing patients
- [ ] Site uses HTTPS with valid SSL certificate
- [ ] Privacy policy is accessible and addresses health information handling
- [ ] Google Analytics and Google Search Console installed and monitored

## SEO & Content

> **Why it matters:** Patients search for conditions, symptoms, and treatments before they search for a provider. Educational content that ranks builds trust and drives organic patient acquisition at zero marginal cost.

- [ ] Service pages target "[condition/service] + [city]" keywords (e.g., "teeth whitening Springfield", "knee replacement Dallas")
- [ ] Provider pages target "[specialty] + [city]" keywords (e.g., "pediatric dentist Springfield", "therapist near me Dallas")
- [ ] FAQ content targets real patient questions ("does insurance cover [service]", "what to expect during [procedure]", "how long does [treatment] take")
- [ ] Blog or health education section publishes regularly (monthly minimum) with original, useful content
- [ ] Content avoids making medical claims that could trigger regulatory issues
- [ ] Schema markup implemented: MedicalBusiness, Physician, MedicalCondition, MedicalProcedure, FAQPage
- [ ] LocalBusiness schema includes correct NAP, hours, and geo-coordinates
- [ ] Google Search Console monitored for health-related query performance
- [ ] Title tags and meta descriptions optimized for local + service intent
- [ ] Internal linking connects condition pages to relevant provider pages and booking CTAs
- [ ] Image alt text is descriptive and includes relevant terms naturally
- [ ] Content is reviewed for medical accuracy by a licensed provider before publishing

## Paid Acquisition

> **Why it matters:** Healthcare paid search captures high-intent patients actively seeking care. Done right, it delivers immediate patient volume. Done wrong, it wastes budget on unqualified clicks or violates ad platform health policies.

- [ ] Google Ads active for high-intent terms ("[specialty] near me", "[condition] treatment [city]", "emergency [service] [city]")
- [ ] Google LSA (Local Services Ads) active where available for the healthcare category
- [ ] Ad copy complies with Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy (no misleading claims, required disclaimers)
- [ ] Meta Ads use HIPAA-safe targeting only — no custom audiences from patient lists without a BAA and patient authorization
- [ ] No retargeting campaigns that imply a health condition ("we noticed you looked at our diabetes page" is a HIPAA and platform policy violation)
- [ ] Retargeting is limited to general brand awareness or broad service categories
- [ ] Dedicated landing pages exist for each ad group with message match (ad promise matches page content)
- [ ] Landing pages include booking CTA above the fold
- [ ] Call tracking does not record PHI without documented patient consent
- [ ] Ad extensions configured: location, call, sitelinks, structured snippets
- [ ] Negative keyword list maintained to exclude irrelevant searches (job seekers, DIY treatments, unrelated conditions)
- [ ] Geographic targeting is set appropriately for the practice's service area
- [ ] Budget allocation prioritizes highest-converting services and highest-LTV patient types
- [ ] Weekly performance review covers CPA, conversion rate, and lead quality (not just clicks)

## Email & Patient Communication

> **Why it matters:** Patient communication drives retention, recall revenue, and reactivation. A dental patient seen twice a year for 20 years is worth thousands. Automated reminders prevent attrition from forgetfulness, not dissatisfaction.

- [ ] Appointment reminders sent via text and email (day before + morning of)
- [ ] Recall/recare reminders automated for recurring visits (dental cleanings every 6 months, annual physicals, vision exams, etc.)
- [ ] Educational newsletters sent periodically (seasonal health tips, new services, provider introductions)
- [ ] Email platform is HIPAA-compliant with a signed BAA
- [ ] Opt-in consent documented for every patient receiving marketing emails
- [ ] No PHI included in email subject lines (subject lines are not encrypted and may be previewed)
- [ ] Email content does not reference specific patient conditions or treatment history
- [ ] Re-engagement campaign active for patients not seen in 12+ months
- [ ] Unsubscribe mechanism works and is honored immediately
- [ ] Patient communication preferences (text vs. email vs. phone) are captured and respected

## Social Media

> **Why it matters:** Social media humanizes the practice and builds familiarity. Patients choose providers they feel they already know. The goal is trust and personality, not viral reach.

- [ ] Facebook and Instagram presence active with consistent posting (2-3x per week minimum)
- [ ] No patient photos or stories posted without explicit written consent (separate from HIPAA treatment consent)
- [ ] Content calendar includes health awareness month topics (Heart Health Month, Dental Health Month, Mental Health Awareness Month, etc.)
- [ ] Provider personality content published regularly (staff introductions, behind-the-scenes, day-in-the-life, community involvement)
- [ ] Community involvement and sponsorship content posted
- [ ] Office culture content shared (team celebrations, new equipment, office upgrades)
- [ ] No medical advice given in comments or DMs — all clinical questions directed to the office for proper consultation
- [ ] Comment moderation active to remove spam and flag potential HIPAA issues (patients self-disclosing in comments)
- [ ] Social media policy exists and staff are trained on what they can and cannot post

---

## Referral & Retention

> **Why it matters:** Healthcare practices live and die by referrals — both provider-to-provider and patient-to-patient. A strong referral network compounds over years. Retention is cheaper than acquisition by an order of magnitude.

- [ ] Provider-to-provider referral network actively maintained (specialists referring to generalists and vice versa)
- [ ] Referral relationships tracked (which providers refer patients to you, and volume)
- [ ] Thank-you or acknowledgment process exists for referring providers
- [ ] Patient referral program in place where permitted (check state regulations — some states restrict patient referral incentives for healthcare)
- [ ] Referral program complies with federal Anti-Kickback Statute and Stark Law where applicable
- [ ] Patient satisfaction surveys sent post-visit (automated)
- [ ] Net Promoter Score (NPS) tracked over time
- [ ] Negative survey responses trigger a follow-up before the patient leaves a public review
- [ ] Patient loyalty or membership program exists for elective/cosmetic services where permitted (dental membership plans, med spa packages)
- [ ] Internal referral tracking for multi-specialty practices (e.g., GP referring to in-house dermatology)
- [ ] Annual patient retention rate is measured and benchmarked

### CRITICAL: HIPAA Override Rule

**If the HIPAA Compliance & Medical Advertising Rules section scores 2 or below (out of 5), the entire audit is flagged as CRITICAL RISK regardless of the total score.** A healthcare business can score perfectly on every other section, but a HIPAA failure exposes the practice to federal fines, lawsuits, loss of licensure, and reputational destruction that no marketing can repair.

When HIPAA scores 1-2:
- Total score is overridden and reported as **CRITICAL RISK**
- The audit report must lead with HIPAA remediation as the #1 priority
- No other marketing work should proceed until HIPAA compliance gaps are closed
- Recommend immediate consultation with a healthcare compliance attorney

## Exclusions

This checklist does **not** cover:

- Hospital systems or large health network marketing
- Pharmaceutical or medical device manufacturer marketing (FDA-regulated)
- Health insurance company marketing
- Clinical trial recruitment
- Multi-state telehealth-only practices (licensing complexity exceeds this scope)
- EHR/EMR system selection or configuration
- Medical billing and coding optimization
