# Professional Services (B2B) Audit Checklist

> **Use when:** Auditing a professional services firm where trust, credentials, and relationships drive the buying decision. Best fit for law firms, accounting firms, management consultants, marketing agencies, financial advisors, architects, engineering firms, IT services firms, and similar B2B or B2C professional services with long sales cycles and high client lifetime value.

---

## Thought Leadership & Content Authority

> **Why it matters:** Professional services firms sell expertise. Prospective clients evaluate credibility before they ever talk to you. Publishing original thinking is the single most effective way to differentiate from competitors who offer the same services at the same price.

- [ ] Publishing cadence is defined and consistent (minimum 2x/month for blog or articles)
- [ ] Firm produces original research, surveys, or proprietary data at least annually
- [ ] Key partners/principals have bylined articles in industry publications
- [ ] White papers or in-depth guides exist for each major practice area
- [ ] Firm leaders are speaking at industry conferences or local business events
- [ ] Podcast guesting strategy exists (appearing on relevant industry or business podcasts)
- [ ] Firm publishes or contributes to a regular podcast or video series
- [ ] LinkedIn articles are published by individual practitioners, not just the company page
- [ ] Content addresses specific client pain points rather than generic industry overviews
- [ ] Each piece of thought leadership includes a clear next step (consultation, download, subscribe)
- [ ] Content is repurposed across channels (blog post becomes LinkedIn article becomes email becomes social)
- [ ] Firm maintains a "newsroom" or insights section on the website that is current (no posts older than 90 days on the first page)
- [ ] Regulatory or market change commentary is published within 2 weeks of major developments
- [ ] Case-based teaching content exists (anonymized client scenarios showing methodology)

## LinkedIn Presence

> **Why it matters:** LinkedIn is the primary professional discovery platform. For B2B professional services, it is often more valuable than the website itself. Personal brands of partners and senior staff drive more engagement than company pages.

- [ ] Company page is fully completed: logo, banner, description, specialties, location, employee count
- [ ] Company page description clearly states who the firm serves and what outcomes it delivers
- [ ] Company page posts at least 3x/week with a mix of thought leadership, firm news, and engagement content
- [ ] Founder/managing partner has an optimized personal LinkedIn profile with a clear headline (not just job title)
- [ ] At least 3 senior practitioners have active, optimized LinkedIn profiles
- [ ] Employee advocacy program exists: staff are encouraged and enabled to share firm content
- [ ] LinkedIn articles are published by individuals (not just the company page) at least 2x/month
- [ ] LinkedIn newsletter is active with a growing subscriber base
- [ ] Posts use a mix of formats: text, carousel/document, video, polls
- [ ] Key staff engage in relevant LinkedIn groups and comment on industry conversations
- [ ] Connection strategy targets ideal client profiles, referral partners, and industry peers
- [ ] LinkedIn SSI (Social Selling Index) is tracked for key business developers
- [ ] Company page analytics are reviewed monthly (follower growth, engagement rate, visitor demographics)
- [ ] Firm leaders comment on and share client/partner content to build reciprocal engagement
- [ ] Showcase pages exist for distinct practice areas or service lines if the firm is large enough

## Referral Network & Partnerships

> **Why it matters:** Referrals are the dominant source of new business for most professional services firms. A firm without a structured referral program is leaving its largest growth lever to chance.

- [ ] Formal referral program exists with clear process for requesting, tracking, and acknowledging referrals
- [ ] Referral sources are tracked in CRM with attribution to originating partner or firm
- [ ] Strategic partnerships exist with complementary (non-competing) firms (e.g., law firm + accounting firm, consultant + software vendor)
- [ ] Co-marketing activities with partners happen at least quarterly (joint webinars, co-authored content, shared events)
- [ ] Alumni network is maintained and engaged (former employees, former clients)
- [ ] Professional association memberships are active with regular participation (not just listed)
- [ ] Firm representatives hold leadership roles in relevant professional associations or chambers
- [ ] Referral thank-you process is systematic (acknowledgment within 48 hours, outcome update, reciprocation)
- [ ] Cross-referral agreements exist with firms in adjacent specialties
- [ ] Referral sources receive periodic value (insights, event invitations, introductions) independent of active referrals
- [ ] Firm tracks referral conversion rate and average value of referred clients vs. other sources
- [ ] Annual referral source review identifies top sources for deeper relationship investment

## Credentials & Trust Signals

> **Why it matters:** Professional services buyers use credentials as a shortcut for quality evaluation. The absence of visible credentials creates doubt, even if the work is excellent.

- [ ] Industry certifications and licenses are prominently displayed on website and profiles (CPA, JD, PE, PMP, CFP, etc.)
- [ ] Professional designations are current and verifiable
- [ ] Awards and recognitions are listed with year and granting organization
- [ ] Case studies exist with named clients where permitted (minimum 3, target 6+)
- [ ] Case studies follow Problem-Solution-Results structure with quantified outcomes
- [ ] Client logos are displayed on homepage or dedicated clients page (with permission)
- [ ] Speaking engagements and conference appearances are listed with dates and topics
- [ ] Media mentions, press features, and expert commentary are collected and displayed
- [ ] Industry-specific directory profiles are claimed and optimized:
  - [ ] Legal: Avvo, Martindale-Hubbell, Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers, FindLaw
  - [ ] Accounting: CPA Directory, Accounting Today
  - [ ] Consulting/Agencies: Clutch, G2, GoodFirms, UpCity
  - [ ] Financial: NAPFA, CFP Board, BrokerCheck
  - [ ] Architecture/Engineering: AIA directory, ACEC directory
- [ ] Firm bio includes founding year, team size, and notable milestones
- [ ] Professional liability/E&O insurance is current and can be referenced if clients ask
- [ ] Memberships and affiliations section exists on the website with logos
- [ ] Testimonials on the website include the person's full name, title, and company where permitted
- [ ] Board seats, advisory roles, and pro bono leadership are documented

## Website & Conversion

> **Why it matters:** The website is where referral warm leads, LinkedIn visitors, and search traffic go to validate credibility and take the next step. It must convey authority and make it easy to start a conversation.

- [ ] Each partner/principal has a bio page with professional photo, credentials, areas of focus, and personality (not just a resume)
- [ ] Bio pages include direct contact information or a scheduling link for that individual
- [ ] Every major practice area or service line has a dedicated, in-depth page (not just a bullet in a list)
- [ ] Service pages explain the process, typical outcomes, and who the service is for
- [ ] Case results, project portfolio, or representative matters section exists
- [ ] Consultation or engagement booking flow is frictionless (2 clicks or fewer from any page)
- [ ] Phone number is prominent in the header on every page with a clickable `tel:` link
- [ ] KaiCalls AI receptionist (`kaicalls.com`) is configured to handle missed calls, after-hours calls, and initial consultation scheduling — professional services clients expect to reach a person when they call
- [ ] KaiCalls is trained on the firm's practice areas, common intake questions, and scheduling process
- [ ] After-hours calls route to KaiCalls instead of voicemail
- [ ] Overflow calls during business hours route to KaiCalls when staff cannot answer
- [ ] Gated resources exist for lead capture (white papers, guides, checklists, templates)
- [ ] Lead capture forms ask for enough qualifying information without creating friction (name, company, need — not 15 fields)
- [ ] Contact form submissions trigger an auto-response confirming receipt and expected response time
- [ ] Site clearly communicates geographic service area and any jurisdictional limitations
- [ ] Mobile experience is clean and loads in under 3 seconds
- [ ] HTTPS is active across the entire site
- [ ] Google Analytics and Google Search Console are installed and verified
- [ ] Conversion tracking is configured for form submissions, phone calls, and consultation bookings

## Email & Nurture

> **Why it matters:** Professional services have long sales cycles. Email keeps the firm top-of-mind during the months (or years) between first contact and engagement. It is also the best channel for deepening relationships with existing clients.

- [ ] Email newsletter exists for prospects and clients (monthly minimum)
- [ ] Newsletter content provides genuine value (regulatory updates, market insights, practical guidance) rather than self-promotion
- [ ] Drip sequence exists for prospects who inquired but did not book a consultation
- [ ] Post-consultation nurture sequence exists for prospects who consulted but did not engage
- [ ] Client update emails go out when regulations, laws, or market conditions change that affect them
- [ ] Re-engagement sequence exists for past clients (annual check-in, service anniversary, or trigger-based)
- [ ] Seasonal or deadline-driven emails are scheduled (tax deadlines, compliance dates, renewal periods)
- [ ] Email list is segmented by at least: prospect vs. client, practice area interest, and industry vertical
- [ ] CRM integration exists so email activity syncs with contact records
- [ ] Email open rates, click rates, and unsubscribe rates are monitored monthly
- [ ] Unsubscribe and opt-out process complies with CAN-SPAM and applicable regulations
- [ ] Email design is professional and matches firm branding (no generic templates)
- [ ] Partner or practitioner personal emails supplement firm-wide sends for key relationships

## SEO & Local Visibility

> **Why it matters:** When a business needs a new accountant, attorney, or consultant, the first move is often a search query. Practice area pages targeting "[service] + [city]" capture high-intent traffic that referrals alone cannot reach.

- [ ] Dedicated practice area pages target "[service] [city]" keywords (e.g., "business litigation attorney Dallas")
- [ ] Each practice area page includes substantial, unique content (not thin doorway pages)
- [ ] Google Business Profile is claimed, verified, and fully optimized
- [ ] GBP categories are set correctly (primary and secondary)
- [ ] GBP posts are published regularly (minimum 2x/month)
- [ ] GBP photos include office, team, events, and professional context
- [ ] NAP (name, address, phone) is consistent across website, GBP, and all directory listings
- [ ] Professional directories are submitted and profiles are complete (see Credentials section for platform list)
- [ ] FAQ content exists targeting long-tail questions prospects ask ("How much does a business valuation cost?", "What happens during a tax audit?")
- [ ] FAQ pages use proper FAQ schema markup
- [ ] ProfessionalService, Attorney, AccountingService, or relevant schema markup is implemented (JSON-LD)
- [ ] Individual practitioner schema (Person) is implemented on bio pages
- [ ] Blog content targets informational queries in the firm's practice areas
- [ ] Internal linking connects blog posts to relevant service pages
- [ ] Title tags and meta descriptions are unique and keyword-targeted on every page
- [ ] Site has an XML sitemap submitted to Google Search Console
- [ ] Core Web Vitals pass (LCP, FID/INP, CLS within Google thresholds)

## Reviews & Reputation

> **Why it matters:** Reviews are the second opinion prospects seek after a referral or website visit. A thin review profile raises doubt; a strong one closes the credibility gap.

- [ ] Google review count is competitive for the market and practice area
- [ ] Average Google rating is 4.5 or higher
- [ ] Review generation process exists (systematic ask after successful engagement or milestone)
- [ ] Every Google review receives a professional response within 72 hours
- [ ] Negative reviews receive calm, empathetic, offline-resolution responses (no defensive tone, no confidential details)
- [ ] Industry-specific review platforms are active:
  - [ ] Legal: Avvo reviews, Google, Martindale-Hubbell client reviews
  - [ ] Consulting/Agency: Clutch reviews, G2 reviews, GoodFirms
  - [ ] Financial: Google, Yelp, NerdWallet, SmartAsset
  - [ ] Medical (if applicable): Healthgrades, Vitals, ZocDoc
- [ ] Testimonials are displayed on the website with attribution (name, title, company where permitted)
- [ ] Case study pipeline exists to turn successful engagements into publishable stories
- [ ] Reputation monitoring is active (Google Alerts, review platform notifications, or a monitoring tool)
- [ ] Team is trained on ethical review solicitation rules for their profession (many bar associations and licensing boards have rules)

## Paid Acquisition

> **Why it matters:** Paid channels supplement referrals and organic with predictable, scalable lead flow. For professional services, the high client lifetime value means even expensive clicks can deliver strong ROI.

- [ ] Google Ads campaigns target high-intent keywords ("[service] near me", "[service] [city]", "best [professional] for [need]")
- [ ] Google Ads landing pages are service-specific (not homepage)
- [ ] Negative keyword list is maintained to exclude irrelevant traffic (jobs, DIY, free, salary, etc.)
- [ ] LinkedIn Ads are running for B2B targeting (job title, company size, industry filters)
- [ ] LinkedIn ad creative uses thought leadership content (articles, white papers) rather than direct hard-sell
- [ ] Retargeting campaigns exist for website visitors who did not convert
- [ ] Google Local Services Ads (LSA) are active if the firm's category is eligible
- [ ] LSA leads are responded to within 5 minutes (KaiCalls handles overflow)
- [ ] Budget is allocated between brand terms (defensive) and non-brand terms (acquisition)
- [ ] Cost per lead and cost per acquired client are tracked by channel and campaign
- [ ] Ad copy complies with platform policies and professional ethics rules (no guarantees of outcomes for legal, no misleading claims for financial)
- [ ] Monthly performance review covers spend, leads, CPL, conversion rate, and client acquisition cost
- [ ] A/B testing is active on ad copy and landing pages

## Client Retention & Expansion

> **Why it matters:** Acquiring a new client costs 5-7x more than retaining one. Existing clients are the most likely source of repeat engagements, expanded scope, and referrals. Neglecting them is the most expensive marketing failure.

- [ ] Systematic check-in cadence exists for active clients (quarterly at minimum, monthly for ongoing engagements)
- [ ] Cross-sell and upsell opportunities are identified and tracked per client
- [ ] Client satisfaction is measured (NPS survey, satisfaction survey, or structured feedback conversations)
- [ ] Client advisory board or council exists for the firm's most strategic clients
- [ ] Annual business reviews are conducted for top-tier clients
- [ ] Client appreciation events are held at least annually (holiday event, client dinner, golf outing, educational seminar)
- [ ] Client success stories and wins are celebrated internally and externally (with permission)
- [ ] Relationship ownership is clear: every client has a named relationship partner
- [ ] Transition process exists when a relationship partner departs (warm handoff, not cold reassignment)
- [ ] Client feedback is routed back to service delivery teams for continuous improvement
- [ ] At-risk client indicators are defined and monitored (engagement decline, missed meetings, slow payment)
- [ ] Win-back process exists for clients who have disengaged or churned

## Exclusions

This checklist does **not** cover:

- [ ] Industry-specific regulatory compliance for marketing (bar association advertising rules, SEC marketing rules, FINRA compliance, etc.) — consult the firm's compliance officer
- [ ] Multi-office or international firm coordination
- [ ] Recruitment marketing and employer branding
- [ ] Internal communications and knowledge management
- [ ] Pricing strategy and fee structure optimization
- [ ] Technology stack selection beyond marketing tools
