# Growth Distribution Engine (0 → $5M ARR Channel Hacking)

> **Use when:** A company has a working product but no repeatable way to reach buyers. This is the operating system for the first marketing hire — the distribution hacker who tests channels, finds the 1-2 that scale, and goes all-in. Channel-agnostic strategy here; the channel guides hold the tactics.

## The Distribution-First Thesis

Most startups die from a distribution problem, not a product problem. They build something people would use and never solve how those people find it. After 20+ company investments, the pattern repeats: **the highest-impact first marketing hire is a distribution hacker, not a brand marketer.**

The job is narrow and brutal. Take the company from $0 to $5M ARR by finding the **1-2 channels that actually scale** for this specific business, then going all-in. Everything else is noise.

**Product quality sets the ceiling. Distribution decides whether you reach it.** A mediocre product with a solved channel beats a great product nobody can find.

### Why distribution kills startups

| Failure | What it looks like | Root cause |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| **No channel** | "We built it, growth is flat" | Never ran disciplined channel tests |
| **Wrong channel** | "We're doing what worked for [competitor]" | Copied a channel that fits their economics, not yours |
| **Too many channels** | "We're on 8 platforms, all underperforming" | Spread so thin no channel got a real test |
| **Premature scale** | "It worked, then CAC tripled" | Scaled past the channel's ceiling |
| **Quit too early** | "We tried SEO for 6 weeks, nothing" | Killed a slow channel before its learning curve |

### The first marketing hire: distribution hacker profile

Hire a **generalist who ships**, not a specialist who plans.

| Trait | What it means | Anti-pattern |
|-------|--------------|--------------|
| **Generalist** | Comfortable running email, ads, content, outbound, and partnerships in the same week | Channel specialist who only knows paid social |
| **Ships fast** | Launches a test in days, not a strategy deck in weeks | Wants a 6-month brand platform first |
| **Quantitative** | Reads CAC, payback, and leading indicators without a dashboard team | Reports impressions and "engagement" |
| **Scrappy** | Does things that don't scale to get the first signal | Insists on tooling and headcount before testing |
| **Killer instinct** | Cuts losing channels without ego | Defends a pet channel past the data |
| **Customer-obsessed** | Talks to buyers weekly, reads the language they use | Optimizes funnels without ever hearing a sales call |

**Who NOT to hire first:** a brand marketer, a "Head of Marketing" with a team-building mandate, an agency, or a specialist who only knows one channel. Hire those after a channel is proven — never before.

> Founders run distribution until the first hire. The hire inherits a system, not a blank page. See the first-90-days plan at the end of this file.

## Channel/Market Fit

Every business has roughly **1-2 channels that scale and a long tail that wastes money.** Channel/market fit is the match between how your product creates value, who buys it, and where those buyers can be reached at a cost your economics allow.

The mistake is treating all 19 channels as equally viable. They are not. The job is to **find the fit fast** — burn the least money proving which 1-2 channels carry the company.

### The Bullseye Framework (Traction, Weinberg & Mares)

The canonical method for finding channel/market fit. Three rings:

```
OUTER RING  — Brainstorm: every channel could plausibly work. List all of them.
MIDDLE RING — Test: pick 3 promising channels, run cheap real tests in parallel.
INNER RING  — Focus: the one channel showing traction gets all the resources.
```

The 19 traction channels from *Traction*: viral marketing, PR, unconventional PR, SEM, social/display ads, offline ads, SEO, content marketing, email marketing, engineering as marketing, target market blogs, business development, sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements, community building.

**Modernized for 2026.** The 19 still map, but the surface has shifted. Add and re-weight:

| Old framing | 2026 reality |
|-------------|-------------|
| SEO | SEO **+ AEO** — answer-engine visibility across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI ([`frameworks/aeo-ai-search/aeo-ai-search-playbook-2026.md`](../frameworks/aeo-ai-search/aeo-ai-search-playbook-2026.md)) |
| Cold email / sales | **AI outbound** — research, personalization, and sequencing at volume ([`channels/ai-outbound.md`](../channels/ai-outbound.md)) |
| Viral / content | **AI UGC** — programmatic short-form creative for B2C ([`channels/ai-ugc.md`](../channels/ai-ugc.md)) |
| Trade shows / offline | **Events & experiential** as a top B2B channel again ([`channels/events-experiential.md`](../channels/events-experiential.md)) |
| Affiliate / influencer | **Creator economy + sponsorships** — newsletters, podcasts, YouTube ([`channels/sponsorships.md`](../channels/sponsorships.md), [`playbooks/influencer-marketing.md`](./influencer-marketing.md)) |

Score every channel on five axes before testing. Definitions:

- **Speed-to-signal** — how fast you learn if it works (days → quarters)
- **Cost-to-test** — minimum spend to get a real read
- **Scale ceiling** — how big it gets before saturating
- **Defensibility** — how hard for competitors to copy your position
- **ACV-fit** — which deal sizes the channel economics support

Scores are **directional benchmarks**, 1 (low) to 5 (high). Calibrate against your own data.

## The Channel-Testing System

Discipline beats intuition. Run channel tests like experiments, not vibes.

### The Rule of 3

Test **3 channels at a time** — not 1, not 10.

- **Test 1 channel** and you can't tell if a bad result is the channel or your execution.
- **Test 10 channels** and none gets enough attention to produce a real signal.
- **Test 3 channels** and you get comparison, parallelism, and focus at once.

```
ROUND 1: Test channels A, B, C in parallel for one cycle.
  → Kill the worst. Keep the best 1-2.
ROUND 2: Double down on the winner. Add ONE new channel to the open slot.
  → Repeat until one channel clearly carries the company.
```

### Designing one channel test

Every test needs five things before it starts:

| Element | Definition | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Hypothesis** | The specific bet, stated as a sentence | "Founder-led LinkedIn posts will produce 5 qualified demo requests in 30 days" |
| **Minimum viable budget/time** | Smallest spend that gives a real read | $1.5-3K paid, or 20-30 hrs of effort for organic |
| **Success metric** | The number that decides | Cost per qualified lead < $150 |
| **Leading indicator** | The early signal that predicts the metric | Reply rate, CTR, hook-rate, demo-show-rate |
| **Time box** | When you decide | Paid: 2-4 weeks. Organic/SEO: 1-3 months |

### Minimum viable test by channel type

| Channel type | Min budget | Min time | Primary leading indicator |
|--------------|-----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Paid search/social | $1.5-5K | 2-4 weeks | CTR, then CPL/CAC |
| AI outbound | $0.5-2K tooling | 3-6 weeks | Positive reply rate (target >3-5%) |
| Organic short-form | Time only | 4-8 weeks | View-to-follow, hook retention |
| LinkedIn founder-led | Time only | 4-8 weeks | Profile views, inbound DMs |
| SEO / AEO | $2-5K content | 3-6 months | Indexed pages ranking, AI citations |
| Events / field | $5-15K | 1-3 events | Booked meetings per event |
| Partnerships | Time + integration | 1-2 quarters | Co-sourced opportunities |

### Kill / double-down criteria

Decide before you start, so the data decides — not your ego.

```
DOUBLE DOWN when:
  - Leading indicator beats benchmark AND trend is up across the time box
  - Unit economics pencil at current scale (channel CAC < CAC ceiling)
  - You can name WHY it works (repeatable, not a fluke)

KILL when:
  - Leading indicator flat or down after the full time box
  - Economics don't pencil even with optimistic optimization
  - The win required heroics that don't repeat

KEEP TESTING (don't kill yet) when:
  - Signal is positive but under-resourced (you starved it)
  - The mechanic worked but the offer/creative was the problem
```

### Dead channel vs under-resourced channel

The most expensive mistake is killing a channel that was starved, or scaling one that was a fluke.

| Signal | Dead channel | Under-resourced channel |
|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| Leading indicator | Flat from day one | Positive but noisy / small sample |
| Effort applied | Full time box, real budget | Half the budget, distracted operator |
| Failure mode | Mechanic fundamentally misfit | Execution or volume too low to read |
| Right move | Kill, log the learning | Re-run with proper resourcing first |

> A channel is not dead until it had a real test: full time box, minimum viable budget, and a competent operator. Log every verdict — and the reasoning — in the [`playbooks/experimentation-ledger.md`](./experimentation-ledger.md).

## Sequencing by Stage

Match channel strategy to revenue stage. The wrong channel at the wrong stage wastes runway. Cross-reference [`playbooks/marketing-by-stage.md`](./marketing-by-stage.md) for the full activity map.

### $0 → first revenue: do things that don't scale

Founder-led. The goal is **learning, not scale.**

- Run 5-10 manual outreaches a day (DM, email, in-person).
- Talk to every customer. Record the exact language they use.
- Hand-build the first 10-50 customers however you can.
- Do NOT hire, do NOT run paid, do NOT start SEO.

**Why manual first:** you're learning the customer and the message. Manual work that teaches you who buys and why is the input every channel that scales depends on.

### $0 → $1M ARR: one channel, deep

Pick ONE channel and commit for 90 days. Find channel/market fit before adding anything.

- Run the Rule of 3 once to find the winner, then go deep on it.
- Resist the second channel. One working channel beats three half-working ones.
- This is where the distribution hire earns their seat.

### $1M → $5M ARR: add a second channel + loops

The first channel is proven and optimized. Now diversify and compound.

- Add a **second channel** — ideally a slow-compounding one (SEO/AEO, content, partnerships) if your first was fast (paid, outbound).
- Build a **growth loop** so usage feeds acquisition ([`playbooks/growth-loops-applied.md`](./growth-loops-applied.md)).
- Layer **paid on top of organic** once you have a proven offer and known CAC ceiling.

### When to layer paid on organic

Add paid only after **all four** are true:

1. An organic channel already converts (you know the message works).
2. You know your CAC ceiling from contribution margin.
3. A test campaign hits CAC under that ceiling at small scale.
4. You have the cash to fund the payback period.

**Paid amplifies a working offer. It does not discover one.** Running paid before product-message fit just buys expensive proof you don't have an offer yet.

### Sequencing by ACV / motion

| Motion | First channels to test | Why |
|--------|----------------------|-----|
| **Low-ACV / PLG** | PLG loop + SEO/AEO + paid social | Self-serve needs volume and search; sales can't pencil |
| **High-ACV / sales-led** | AI outbound + LinkedIn founder-led + events | Few buyers, high value — targeted reach beats broad |
| **B2C / DTC** | Organic short-form + paid social + AI UGC | Impulse discovery, creative volume, margin-funded paid |

## Channel-Market Fit Decision Tree

Pick the FIRST 3 channels to test using the variables that decide fit: ACV, motion, audience location, sales cycle, margin, and content capability.

```
START: What is your motion?

├── B2B, HIGH ACV ($15K+ ACV, sales-led)
│   ├── Buyers concentrated (named ICP, <5K accounts)?
│   │   → TEST: AI outbound + LinkedIn founder-led + events/field
│   └── Buyers diffuse (broad ICP)?
│       → TEST: LinkedIn founder-led + webinars + sponsorships
│
├── B2B, LOW/MID ACV (self-serve to ~$15K, PLG or light-touch)
│   ├── Strong search demand for the problem?
│   │   → TEST: SEO/AEO + paid search + PLG loop
│   └── Weak search demand (new category)?
│       → TEST: founder-led content (LinkedIn/X/YouTube) + AI outbound + communities
│
└── B2C / DTC
    ├── Visual / impulse product, decent margin?
    │   → TEST: organic short-form + paid social + AI UGC
    ├── Considered purchase, research-driven?
    │   → TEST: SEO/AEO + influencers + paid social
    └── Network / social product?
        → TEST: referral/viral loop + organic short-form + influencers
```

**Tiebreakers when two paths fit:**

- **Thin margin** → favor organic and loop-driven channels; paid will not pencil.
- **Long sales cycle** → favor trust-building channels (content, events, founder-led) over pure response channels.
- **No content capability** → favor outbound and paid first; build content muscle in parallel.
- **Strong founder voice** → over-weight founder-led organic; it is the cheapest defensible channel a startup has.

## The Growth-Hacker Operating Cadence

Run distribution as a weekly loop, not a quarterly plan.

### Weekly experiment loop

```
MONDAY     — Review last week's tests. Kill, double-down, or keep per criteria.
             Pick this week's experiments (keep 3 channels live).
TUE-THU    — Ship. Launch creative, send sequences, publish, run tests.
FRIDAY     — Read leading indicators. Log every result in the ledger.
             Write one lesson if a result generalizes.
```

### Metrics that matter by stage

| Stage | Watch this | Ignore this |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| $0 → first rev | Reply rate, conversation count, customer language | Impressions, followers |
| $0 → $1M | Channel CAC, conversion rate, payback | Brand awareness, vanity reach |
| $1M → $5M | Blended CAC, LTV:CAC, channel mix, loop K-factor | Single-touch attribution as truth |

### Avoid shiny-object channel-hopping

The deadliest habit is abandoning a channel mid-learning-curve for the next exciting platform.

- Commit to the **full time box** before judging any channel.
- A new channel must beat the bar set by your current best — not just look novel.
- Log the urge to switch as a hypothesis, then test it against the data, not the hype cycle.

> Use [`playbooks/experimentation-ledger.md`](./experimentation-ledger.md) as the single source of truth. Every test gets a row: hypothesis, budget, time box, leading indicator, verdict, and lesson. The ledger is what stops channel-hopping — it makes the cost of quitting early visible.

## Unit Economics Gates

A channel that works mechanically still fails if the math doesn't. Gate every channel against your economics before scaling.

### The core ratios

| Metric | Formula | Healthy benchmark |
|--------|---------|------------------|
| **CAC** | Fully-loaded spend ÷ new customers | Channel-specific |
| **Contribution margin** | Revenue − variable costs | Sets the CAC ceiling |
| **CAC payback** | CAC ÷ monthly contribution margin | < 12 months (< 6 ideal for self-serve) |
| **LTV:CAC** | Lifetime value ÷ CAC | > 3:1 |

### The CAC ceiling test

```
CAC CEILING = (First-year contribution margin per customer) ÷ (target payback in years)

A channel PASSES if: channel-level CAC < CAC ceiling at the volume you need.
A channel FAILS if: CAC only pencils at tiny volume, or rises past the ceiling as you scale.
```

**Bold the test:** **can this channel acquire customers below your CAC ceiling at the scale you need — not just at the scale you tested?** Many channels look great at $1K spend and break at $50K as you exhaust the cheap audience (the law of shitty clickthroughs — response rates decay as a channel saturates).

### Blended vs channel-level CAC

- **Channel-level CAC** tells you which channel to fund. Use it for decisions.
- **Blended CAC** (all spend ÷ all customers) tells you company health. Use it for the board.
- Never hide a failing channel inside a healthy blended number. A great organic channel can mask a paid channel burning cash.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure mode | What it looks like | The fix |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Spreading across 10 channels** | Everything is half-resourced, nothing reads clearly | Rule of 3. Kill down to 1-2 |
| **Hiring an agency before finding the channel** | Outsourcing a problem you haven't solved | Find the channel in-house first, then scale it with help |
| **Copying a competitor's channel** | "It works for them" — but their ACV/margin differs | Re-derive channel fit from YOUR economics |
| **Abandoning before the learning curve** | Quit SEO at 6 weeks, paid at 4 days | Honor the full time box per channel type |
| **Scaling past the ceiling** | Worked at $5K, CAC tripled at $50K | Watch marginal CAC; saturating channels need a second channel, not more spend |
| **Hiring a brand person first** | Beautiful deck, no pipeline | Hire the distribution hacker first; brand comes after a channel works |
| **Paid before product-message fit** | Burning ad spend to learn the offer | Use organic/manual to find the message; paid amplifies it |
| **Vanity metrics** | Reporting reach and followers | Report CAC, payback, qualified pipeline |

## First 90 Days for the Distribution Hire

A concrete onboarding plan for the first marketing hire. The goal by day 90 is **one channel showing real traction with economics that pencil.**

### Days 1-30: Learn and instrument

- Sit on 10+ sales/customer calls. Capture the exact buyer language.
- Read every past marketing experiment; rebuild or start the [`playbooks/experimentation-ledger.md`](./experimentation-ledger.md).
- Define the ICP, ACV, contribution margin, and CAC ceiling with finance.
- Run the channel-market fit decision tree. Pick the first 3 channels.
- Ship the first lightweight test in each of the 3 channels (Rule of 3).

### Days 31-60: Test and read

- Run all 3 channel tests to their full time box.
- Track leading indicators weekly; log every result.
- Kill the clear loser. Identify the 1-2 channels with real signal.
- Re-run any under-resourced channel with proper budget before judging.

### Days 61-90: Focus and prove

- Pour resources into the winning channel. Optimize the offer, creative, and funnel.
- Prove channel-level CAC sits below the CAC ceiling at meaningful volume.
- Document the channel as a repeatable system another operator could run.
- Write the case for the second channel — to test next, not now.

### Day 90 success criteria

- [ ] One channel produces customers predictably, within CAC ceiling
- [ ] Leading indicators and the kill/scale criteria are documented
- [ ] The experiment ledger is current and tells the full test story
- [ ] A clear recommendation exists for which channel to test next
- [ ] Buyer language and ICP evidence are captured, not assumed

> The distribution hire's first win is not a campaign. It is a **proven channel and a documented system** that survives their vacation. Build the machine, not the heroics.
