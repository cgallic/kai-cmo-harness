# Sponsorships (Audience Buying)

> **Use when:** Buying access to someone else's audience — newsletters, podcasts, YouTube creators, communities, events — to scale a company toward $5M ARR. Buyer-side only (you are the brand paying for placement). For running your own newsletter or podcast, read `channels/newsletter-strategy.md` and `channels/podcast.md` instead.

---

## Why Sponsorships Belong in the 0→$5M Engine

Sponsorships buy distribution you have not earned yet. **They convert cash into someone else's trust.** That is the whole trade.

- **Borrowed trust** — the host vouches for you, so cold becomes warm in one read.
- **Pre-aggregated audience** — niche newsletters and shows already did the targeting you would pay Meta to approximate.
- **Defensible at small scale** — competitors with bigger ad budgets cannot outbid you for a 4,000-sub niche list they have never heard of.
- **Arbitrage exists** — under-monetized creators sell attention below its true conversion value. Find those before they raise rates.
- **Compounds with owned channels** — a sponsored read drives email signups, and email is the channel you keep. See `playbooks/growth-distribution-engine.md`.

Sponsorships are the **inverse of selling sponsorships in your own newsletter** (`channels/newsletter-strategy.md`). Everything a publisher optimizes to charge more, you scrutinize to pay less.

Position sponsorships inside the broader distribution map: `playbooks/b2b-distribution-playbook.md` for B2B, `playbooks/b2c-distribution-playbook.md` for B2C, `playbooks/influencer-marketing.md` for creator-led deals.

## Sponsorship Types

### Newsletter Sponsorships

| Placement | What It Is | Relative CTR | Typical Use |
|-----------|-----------|:------------:|-------------|
| **Dedicated send** | Whole email is your message | Highest | Launches, high-ticket B2B, webinars |
| **Primary / top placement** | First ad slot, above the fold | High | Core acquisition, flagship offer |
| **Secondary / mid placement** | Mid-body ad slot | Medium | Cheaper test inventory |
| **Classifieds / text link** | One line in a list of links | Low | Cheap reach, retargeting fuel |
| **Sponsored section / takeover** | A recurring branded segment | Medium-High | Multi-issue recurring deals |

Buy a **dedicated send only after a primary placement test proves the list converts.** Dedicated sends cost the most and fatigue the list fastest.

### Podcast Sponsorships

| Format | What It Is | Conversion Signal | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| **Host-read mid-roll** | Host reads your copy ~30-50% into the episode | Strongest | Listeners are committed by mid-roll |
| **Host-read pre-roll** | Read in the first 1-2 minutes | Medium | High reach, easy to skip |
| **Host-read post-roll** | Read at the end | Lowest | Cheapest, weakest |
| **Produced / announcer spot** | Pre-recorded, no host voice | Weak | Treat like a radio ad, avoid early on |

**Baked-in vs dynamic insertion:**

- **Baked-in** — ad lives in the audio file forever. You pay once, get the back-catalog tail. Hard to track, hard to remove.
- **Dynamic (DAI)** — ad served at download time, swappable, capped by date/geo. Easier to measure and pace. Default to dynamic for tests.

Prefer **host-read mid-roll, dynamically inserted, for a first podcast test.**

### YouTube Integrations

| Format | What It Is | Length | When |
|--------|-----------|--------|------|
| **Dedicated video** | Whole video about your product | Full video | High-consideration, expensive, high intent |
| **Integration** | 30/60/90s segment inside a normal video | 30-90s | Default for most tests |
| **End-card / outro mention** | Quick mention after main content | <20s | Cheap, low conversion |
| **Pinned comment + description link** | Add-on to any of the above | — | Always negotiate this in |

Buy a **60-90s integration before a dedicated video.** Negotiate the **pinned comment and a description link with your UTM** into every deal — those drive most of the trackable clicks.

### Creator / IG / TikTok Brand Deals

Short-form creator deals overlap heavily with influencer marketing. Use `playbooks/influencer-marketing.md` for tiers, vetting, brief structure, and contract must-haves. Treat these as audience-buys when the goal is **distribution to the creator's followers**, not UGC you own.

- **IG:** in-feed Reel, Story frames (with link sticker), or carousel.
- **TikTok:** in-feed video; pair with Spark Ads rights to amplify winners. See `channels/tiktok-shop.md` for commerce.
- Always secure **paid-partnership label** and **usage rights** in writing.

### Community Sponsorships (Slack / Discord / Circle)

| Asset | What It Is | Best For |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Pinned announcement** | Founder/admin posts your offer | Launches, exclusive deals |
| **Sponsored channel / role** | Branded space inside the community | Long-term presence, B2B |
| **Newsletter-to-members** | Email to the member list | Direct response |
| **Event/AMA sponsorship** | You host or co-host a session | Demand gen, B2B trust |

Communities convert well for B2B because **the moderator's endorsement carries weight.** Disclosure still applies — a sponsored pin must read as sponsored. See `channels/community-building.md`.

### Event / Conference Sponsorships

| Tier (typical) | Includes | Best For |
|----------------|----------|----------|
| **Booth / table** | Floor space, badge scans | Pipeline, lead capture |
| **Speaking slot** | Stage time, talk or panel | Authority, top-of-funnel B2B |
| **Branding tier** | Logo, lanyard, wifi, swag | Awareness only — weakest ROI |
| **Dinner / side event** | Hosted gathering of attendees | High-intent B2B relationships |

**A hosted side dinner beats a logo on a lanyard for pipeline.** Buy speaking and gatherings before branding. Tie events to `playbooks/event-webinar-marketing.md`.

### Emerging: AI/LLM-Cited Content and Niche Aggregators

- **Sponsoring AI-cited content** — pay for placement in resources that AI answer engines cite (comparison pages, "best X" roundups, structured directories). The bet is on being **named in an LLM answer**, not on direct clicks. Measure with brand-search lift, not CTR.
- **Niche aggregators / directories** — category roundups, "tools for X" lists, vertical marketplaces. Cheap, durable, and often under-priced.
- Treat both as **experimental line items.** Attribution is weakest here; cap spend and judge on incrementality, not last-click. See `playbooks/surround-sound-llm-manipulation.md`.

## Sourcing & Discovery

### Marketplaces and Networks

| Tool | Channel | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Paved** | Newsletters | Largest newsletter marketplace, transparent CPMs |
| **Passionfroot** | Newsletters, creators | Creator-friendly booking pages, good for direct |
| **beehiiv Ad Network** | Newsletters | Programmatic across beehiiv lists, fast to test |
| **Swapstack** | Newsletters | Indie/niche lists, often under-priced |
| **Sponsorship DBs (e.g. Hsecto-style)** | Multi-channel | Searchable inventory + rate data; verify before buying |
| **Spotify Ads / Acast** | Podcasts | Programmatic podcast inventory, dynamic insertion |
| **YouTube BrandConnect** | YouTube | Native brand-creator matching |
| **Direct outreach** | All | Best margins, most work — see template below |
| **Agencies** | Podcast/YouTube | Faster at scale, take 10-20%, less arbitrage |

### Finding Under-Priced ("Arbitrage") Inventory

Find inventory before the creator reprices it. Signals of under-priced slots:

1. **Newsletter under ~20k subs with high open rates** — niche, engaged, often priced on subs not engagement.
2. **Podcast under ~10k downloads/episode with a loud, specific audience** — host-read converts above its CPM.
3. **Creators new to monetization** — flat-fee asks below their conversion value.
4. **Off-marketplace lists** — anyone selling direct, not on Paved, has not been bid up.
5. **Recurring-slot discounts** — book 4 issues, pay for 3.

Move fast on winners. **Lock recurring or exclusive slots before competitors discover the same arbitrage.**

## Vetting an Audience

Never buy on claimed reach alone. Score fit first, then verify the numbers.

### Audience-Fit Score (rate each 1-4, target 12+/16)

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **ICP overlap** | Does the audience match your buyer persona? |
| **Intent proximity** | Are they close to a buying decision in your category? |
| **Trust transfer** | Does the host's credibility map to your offer? |
| **Format fit** | Does the placement format suit your offer's complexity? |

### Real Reach vs Claimed Reach

Request a media kit, then verify against these checks:

| Channel | Healthy Signal | Red Flag |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| **Newsletter** | Open rate 30%+, CTR 1-5%, recent issue screenshots | "Subscribers" with no open rate; open rate >70% (bot-inflated) |
| **Podcast** | Downloads per episode (not total), 7-day windows, consistent trend | "Total downloads ever"; round numbers; no per-episode data |
| **YouTube** | Median views per video, audience-retention graph | Cherry-picked viral outlier; view/sub mismatch |
| **Community** | Weekly active members, message volume | Total signups only; dead channels |

### Fraud and Bot Signals

- **Sudden subscriber/follower spikes** with no content or PR to explain them.
- **Open rates above ~70%** on a large list — usually Apple MPP inflation or bots.
- **Engagement that does not match reach** — 50k subs, 4 comments.
- **No willingness to share past sponsor results** — the loudest red flag.

Always ask: **"Can you share results from your last 2-3 sponsors?"** A confident publisher shares ranges. Reluctance is a signal to walk or test small.

## Pricing & Negotiation

### CPM Benchmarks (directional, 2026 — verify per deal)

| Channel | Typical CPM Range | Notes |
|---------|------------------|-------|
| **Newsletter (consumer)** | $15-40 CPM | Per 1,000 recipients; classifieds far lower |
| **Newsletter (B2B/niche)** | $40-100+ CPM | Narrow, high-value audiences command 2-3x consumer |
| **Podcast (host-read mid-roll)** | $20-50 CPM | Per 1,000 downloads; pre/post-roll lower |
| **Podcast (produced spot)** | $15-25 CPM | Weaker conversion, price accordingly |
| **YouTube integration** | $20-60 CPM | Per 1,000 views; dedicated videos priced higher, often flat |
| **Community pin / send** | Flat-fee | Price against member count and activity |
| **Event speaking/booth** | Flat-fee | Judge on pipeline, not CPM |

These are **benchmarks, not quotes.** Niche, intent, and host trust move them. Treat any number a media kit cites as a starting ask.

### Pricing Models

| Model | How It Works | When to Use |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Flat fee** | Fixed price per placement | Default; predictable; most creator deals |
| **CPM** | Pay per 1,000 delivered impressions | Larger lists/shows; pay for actual delivery |
| **Performance / CPA** | Pay per signup or sale | When the creator believes in your offer; rare for top inventory |
| **Affiliate hybrid** | Lower flat fee + commission on tracked sales | Aligns incentives; needs clean tracking and SKU margin (see `channels/affiliate-referral.md`) |

### Negotiation Levers

1. **Ask for the rate card, then test below it.** First-time, smaller, or off-marketplace creators flex 10-30%.
2. **Bundle for a recurring discount** — buy 3-4 placements, pay for fewer.
3. **Offer to make the creative easy** — a tight brief and assets save the host time and earn goodwill.
4. **Trade exclusivity for price** — category exclusivity for a window can justify a discount or lock a winner.
5. **Pay faster** — net-0 or upfront payment is worth a discount to many indie creators.

### First-Time Test Budget

- Cap a single first test at **$500-2,000** for newsletters/podcasts/creators; less for classifieds.
- Run **2-3 placements in parallel**, not one — single tests are noisy.
- Never buy a recurring or exclusive deal before one tracked placement clears your payback bar.

### Always Secure Before Paying

- **Unique promo code** per placement (e.g. `SHOWNAME20`).
- **UTM-tagged link** per placement (`?utm_source=...&utm_medium=sponsorship&utm_campaign=...`).
- **Dedicated landing page** when budget allows — matches the host's framing, lifts conversion, isolates attribution.
- **Asset delivery date** and **a draft-review window** for the creative.

## Creative & Messaging

### What Makes a Sponsored Read Convert

1. **One offer, one action.** Split attention kills response. Pick a single CTA.
2. **A specific hook in the first line.** "Answer every call in 0.4 seconds" beats "We help businesses grow."
3. **Lead with the audience's pain**, not your feature list.
4. **A reason to act now** — a code expiry, a launch window, a bonus.
5. **The host's own words.** A read that sounds scripted converts worse than one in the host's voice.

### Briefing a Creator Without Killing Authenticity

Give the host the message, not the script. Their authenticity is what you are buying.

```
SPONSORSHIP BRIEF
═════════════════
Brand:         {brand}
Placement:     {host-read mid-roll / newsletter primary / 60s integration}
Air/send date: {date}
Tracking:      Code {CODE} + link {tracked URL}

THE ONE THING (say this in your own words):
  {Single core message — e.g., "Stop losing leads to missed calls"}

WHY YOUR AUDIENCE CARES:
  {1-2 lines of audience-specific pain}

PROOF YOU CAN USE (only what's substantiated):
  - {Specific, verifiable claim}
  - {Specific, verifiable claim}

CTA:
  "{Go to URL / use code CODE for X}"

MUST INCLUDE:
  - Clear sponsorship disclosure (see Disclosure & Compliance)
  - The tracked link and/or code, spoken and in show notes/description

MUST AVOID:
  - Unsubstantiated claims, medical/financial/legal promises
  - Naming competitors disparagingly
  - "Organic"/non-sponsored framing — this IS sponsored

CREATIVE FREEDOM:
  Use your voice and format. Draft review: {Yes/No}. Revisions: {1 max}.
```

### The Dedicated Landing Page

Match the page to the source. A listener of *Show X* should land on a page that references *Show X*. **Mirror the host's framing, honor the offer/code, and strip navigation that leaks the click.** See `playbooks/landing-page-messaging-workflow.md`.

## Measurement & Attribution

### The Attribution-Blindness Problem

Sponsorships are **the hardest paid channel to track.** Listeners hear a podcast in the car and Google you later. Newsletter readers forward the email. Last-click will under-credit sponsorships every time. Plan for blindness; do not pretend it away.

### Tracking Stack (use all that apply)

| Method | Catches | Limitation |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Unique promo code** | Code-redeemers | Misses those who forget the code |
| **Vanity URL** | Direct typers | Few people type URLs |
| **UTM links** | Clickers | Misses delayed/cross-device |
| **"How did you hear about us?"** survey | Self-reported origin | Imperfect recall, but catches dark-social |
| **Pixel / postback** | On-site/app conversions | Only where a click happens |
| **Brand-search lift** | Halo demand | Correlational, needs a baseline |

**Run a post-purchase "how did you hear about us?" survey.** It is the single best catch for sponsorship dark-social that codes and UTMs miss.

### Incrementality and Renewal Decisions

Do not report sponsorship-attributed revenue as incremental profit by default. Codes and UTMs are attribution systems, not proof of lift. For a real read:

- **Contribution margin** by offer, not gross revenue.
- **Incrementality method** — geo holdout, time-staggered placements, or a named directional proxy.
- **Organic baseline** — what brand-search and direct would have done anyway.
- **Refund/cancellation adjustment** and the **payback window** (when CAC is recovered).

### Renew vs Kill

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Tracked CAC under target AND survey corroborates | **Renew, lock recurring slot** |
| Strong survey signal, weak code/UTM | **Renew with better tracking** (dedicated LP, exclusive code) |
| Weak everywhere after a fair test | **Kill, redeploy budget** |
| Mixed; one of 2-3 parallel tests won | **Scale the winner, drop the rest** |

Log every result. Feed winners to `playbooks/what-works.md` and diagnosed losers to `memory/what-doesnt-work.md`.

## Scaling a Sponsorship Portfolio

Treat sponsorships as a portfolio of small bets, most of which you kill.

1. **Test wide, then concentrate.** Run many small tests; reinvest only behind proven slots.
2. **Reinvestment rule** — move budget from a killed test straight into the next test or into scaling a winner. No idle budget.
3. **Lock winners** — secure recurring slots and category exclusivity on the placements that clear payback. Exclusivity protects margin and blocks competitors.
4. **Diversify hosts** — no single creator should carry the channel; audiences and host enthusiasm fatigue.
5. **Refresh creative** — rotate the hook every 3-4 placements on a recurring slot to fight ad blindness.
6. **Mind seasonality** — B2B dips in summer and late December; B2C spikes around its category's peak (holidays, back-to-school, Q1 resolutions). Buy ahead of demand, not into the peak when CPMs rise.

## Disclosure & Compliance (Non-Negotiable)

**Undisclosed paid placement is prohibited.** Every sponsored read, send, video, post, pin, or page that you pay for must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. This is FTC law in the US and has equivalents under UK ASA and EU UCPD. Load `harness/references/creator-disclosure.md` before any deal.

### Hard Rules

1. **Disclose the material connection** — clear language (`Ad`, `Sponsored`, `Paid partnership with {brand}`) where the endorsement appears.
2. **Place disclosure early and visible** — before caption truncation, at the top of the read, in the description's first block. Not buried in a hashtag pile or end-card.
3. **Use native paid-partnership labels** where the platform provides them (YouTube paid-promotion declaration, IG/TikTok branded-content toggle, podcast "this episode is sponsored by").
4. **No fake "organic" framing.** Never let a host pass a paid placement off as an unprompted recommendation. That is undisclosed advertising — stop and fix it.
5. **Affiliate signal too** — when a code or link earns commission, disclose the affiliate relationship in addition to the sponsorship.
6. **The brand owns claim substantiation.** Anything the host states as fact must be substantiated by you. **You cannot outsource a false claim to a creator** — give them only claims you can prove.
7. **Re-check before paid amplification.** Disclosure and usage rights must be re-verified before you boost a creator post (Spark Ads, Partnership Ads). See `playbooks/influencer-marketing.md`.

### Pre-Publish Disclosure Gate

Do not let a placement go live until every item is true:

- [ ] Material-connection disclosure present (`Ad`/`Sponsored`/paid-partnership label) and visible before truncation.
- [ ] Native platform label enabled where available.
- [ ] Affiliate disclosure present when codes/links earn commission.
- [ ] Every claim the host makes is substantiated by brand-provided evidence.
- [ ] No "organic"/non-sponsored framing of paid content.
- [ ] Tracking link/code in the spoken read AND the show notes/description.
- [ ] Evidence row captured (platform, asset/placement ID, disclosure text, label state).

Gate result: **`pass`** = all true; **`hold`** = anything missing. A `hold` does not ship.

Jurisdiction presets (`us_ftc`, `uk_asa`, `eu_ucpd`) and approved disclosure templates live in `harness/references/creator-disclosure.md`.

## Cold-Start Runbook (First 3 Sponsorship Tests)

1. **Define the one metric.** Pick signups or trials or sales — one. Set a target CAC and a payback window.
2. **Source 10-15 candidates** from one marketplace (Paved for newsletters, a niche show list for podcasts) plus 3-5 direct-outreach prospects.
3. **Score fit** with the 12/16 audience-fit rubric. Drop everything below 12.
4. **Vet the top 5** — request media kits, verify open rate / per-episode downloads, ask for last-sponsor results.
5. **Pick 3, run in parallel.** Cap total spend at $1,500-4,000. One placement each, never recurring yet.
6. **Build per-placement tracking** — unique code, UTM link, and one dedicated landing page mirroring each source.
7. **Brief without scripting.** Send the brief template; require disclosure; confirm air/send dates.
8. **Clear the disclosure gate** before each goes live.
9. **Measure for the full payback window** — code redemptions, UTM clicks, AND the "how did you hear" survey. Do not judge on day 1.
10. **Renew/kill** with the table above. Roll dead budget into the next test or into the winner.

### Outreach Template

```
Subject: Sponsoring {Show/Newsletter Name} — quick question

Hi {Name},

I'm with {brand}. We help {ICP} {core outcome}. Your audience of
{specific descriptor} is a strong fit.

I'd like to test one {host-read mid-roll / primary newsletter} placement.

Could you share:
  - Your rate card or current pricing
  - Per-episode downloads / list size + open rate
  - Results from a recent sponsor (range is fine)

If it converts, I'd lock a recurring slot. We provide the brief, a
unique code, and a tracked link — and we handle disclosure correctly.

Open to a date in the next 4-6 weeks?

{Your name}
```

## Portfolio-Scale Runbook (After 3+ Winners)

1. **Codify the winners** — record CAC, payback, and audience traits of every slot that cleared the bar.
2. **Lock recurring + exclusivity** on the top performers before competitors find them.
3. **Systematize sourcing** — keep a live pipeline of vetted candidates; add an agency for podcast/YouTube volume once direct sourcing caps out.
4. **Set a reinvestment cap** — fixed monthly sponsorship budget split: ~60% proven recurring slots, ~30% scaling promising tests, ~10% experimental (AI-cited content, aggregators).
5. **Rotate creative** every 3-4 placements per recurring slot.
6. **Quarterly portfolio review** — kill decayed slots, re-test seasonally, rebalance the 60/30/10 split.
7. **Feed the loop** — winners to `playbooks/what-works.md`, losers diagnosed into `memory/what-doesnt-work.md`, recurring gotchas into `memory/edge-cases.md`.
