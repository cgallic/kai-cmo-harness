# Events & Experiential (IRL Distribution)

> **Use when:** Planning in-person field marketing (conferences, dinners, roadshows, owned events, meetups) or B2C experiential activations (pop-ups, sampling, sponsorship activations, IRL stunts). For virtual webinars and summits, use `playbooks/event-webinar-marketing.md` instead — this file is about bodies in a room.

---

## Where IRL fits the 0→$5M ARR thesis

Events are a **concentration lever**. They trade reach for intensity: fewer people, far higher trust per touch. Pull this lever when your motion needs IRL proof — high-ACV B2B with long cycles, or B2C brands that need word-of-mouth and feed-ready moments to break out of paid-only growth.

| Signal | IRL is worth it | IRL is a trap |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| ACV | $15K+ (B2B) — one deal pays for the event | < $2K self-serve, no human sale |
| Buying committee | 3+ stakeholders, needs in-person trust | Single solo buyer who never meets sales |
| Category | New/complex, needs explanation | Commodity, price-led |
| Word-of-mouth | Drives meaningful share of growth | Irrelevant to acquisition |
| Density | ICP clusters at known events/cities | ICP is globally scattered |

**Run IRL as a pipeline source, not a brand line item.** Tie every event to a meeting count, a pipeline number, or a content-output target before booking it. See `playbooks/growth-distribution-engine.md` for how IRL slots beside owned, earned, and paid channels.

# Part 1 — B2B Field Marketing

## Event types and their ROI profile

| Play | Cost band | Effort | Lead quality | Pipeline speed | Best for |
|------|-----------|--------|--------------|----------------|----------|
| **Attend (badge only)** | $ | Low | Medium | Slow | Hand-picked meetings, recon |
| **Sponsor booth** | $$$–$$$$ | High | Low–Medium | Slow | Brand presence at scale, late-stage |
| **Speaking slot** | $ (free–travel) | Medium | High | Medium | Authority + inbound, cheap reach |
| **Side / satellite event** | $$ | Medium | High | Fast | Capturing a conference's audience cheaply |
| **Intimate dinner (8–14 seats)** | $$ | Medium | Very high | Fast | Early-stage enterprise pipeline |
| **Executive roundtable** | $$ | Medium | Very high | Medium | Multi-account exec relationships |
| **Roadshow (city series)** | $$$ | High | High | Medium | Expanding into new regions |
| **Owned user conference** | $$$$ | Very high | High | Slow | Retention, expansion, community ($3M+ ARR) |
| **Community meetup** | $ | Low–Medium | Medium–High | Slow | Bottom-up/PLG, developer audiences |
| **Hackathon** | $$ | High | Medium | Slow | Developer adoption, recruiting, integrations |

Cost bands: `$` < $2K, `$$` $2–10K, `$$$` $10–40K, `$$$$` $40K+. Treat as directional 2026 benchmarks, not quotes.

## The thesis: don't sponsor the booth, throw the dinner

Buy a meeting, not a banner. A $25K platinum booth at a 5,000-person conference scans a few hundred half-interested badges and sources a handful of real opportunities. A $4K dinner for 12 hand-picked target accounts puts your founder across the table from 12 buyers for two hours.

Why small high-intent gatherings beat big booths before $5M ARR:

1. **Selection beats volume.** You choose the 12 guests. A booth takes whoever walks by.
2. **Context lowers the guard.** People say real things over dinner that they never say at a booth.
3. **The math is friendlier.** Cost-per-qualified-conversation at a curated dinner runs far below a booth's, because there's no swag, no shipping, no booth-staff dead time.
4. **It compounds.** Guests who liked the room invite peers to the next one.

Run booths only when brand presence itself is the goal, the show is your category's anchor event, or you're past $3M ARR with a sales team that can work a high-traffic floor. **Default to the dinner below that line.**

### The 10-person dinner play

Book a private room, not a sponsorship. Run it the night before or during a conference your ICP already attends — you ride their travel budget, not yours.

```
GUEST MATH (target a full table of 10–12 buyers):
  Invite 25–30 target-account contacts  →  expect 35–45% yes
  Over-invite by ~2.5x; dinners no-show at 15–25%
  Mix: 70% prospects, 20% customers (social proof), 10% a "draw" guest

FORMAT:
  - Theme it around a peer problem, NOT your product
    ("How heads of X are handling Y in 2026")
  - Seat a sharp moderator (founder or a respected customer)
  - One structured question to open, then let the table talk
  - Zero pitch. The product comes up because guests ask.

ECONOMICS (directional 2026):
  Venue + F&B: $120–250/head  →  $1.5–3K for a full table
  Outcome target: 5–8 qualified follow-ups from one dinner
  Implied cost-per-qualified-conversation: ~$250–500
```

Use the same skeleton for **executive roundtables** (swap dinner for a hosted 90-minute discussion + lunch) and **roadshows** (repeat the dinner across 3–5 cities in a quarter).

## Pre-event: fill the calendar before you land

Most event ROI is decided before anyone travels. **Walk in with meetings already booked.**

1. **Build the target-account list.** Pull the attendee/exhibitor list (sponsors usually get one; otherwise mine LinkedIn for "attending #EventName" posts). Match against your ICP and named accounts.
2. **Tier the list.** A = active opportunities attending, B = target accounts, C = nice-to-meet. Spend outreach effort A → B → C.
3. **Book before you arrive.** Email and DM 3–4 weeks out. Aim to land 8–15 booked meetings per rep per event.
4. **Recruit the dinner table** from the A/B tiers (see play above).
5. **Pre-warm with content.** Post on LinkedIn that you'll be there and what you're hosting (`channels/linkedin-organic.md`). Let inbound find you.

```
SUBJECT: Grabbing 20 min at {Event}?

Hi {First} — saw {Company}'s going to be at {Event} in {City}.
We're {one-line value tied to their world}.

I'll be there {dates}. Worth 20 minutes on {specific problem
they have}? I can also save you a seat at a small dinner we're
hosting {night} with a few other {their-role} folks.

{Calendar link}
```

## At-event: convert attention to pipeline

**Booth design that actually stops people** (when you do run one):

- **One message, readable in 3 seconds, from 15 feet.** State the outcome, not the category. "Cut missed-call revenue loss" beats "AI Communication Platform."
- **A live demo loop, not a slideshow.** Motion stops people; static logos don't.
- **One reason to stop** — a teardown, a live audit, a working interactive, not a fishbowl of business cards.
- **Clear floor, eye contact, no phones.** Staff standing, facing the aisle, never seated behind a table.

**Qualify fast.** You have 90 seconds. Ask two questions that sort buyer from tourist:

1. "What brought you over?" (intent)
2. "How are you handling {problem} today?" (fit + pain)

Tag every scan on the spot: 🔥 book-now / 🌡️ nurture / ❄️ swag-only. **Route hot leads to a meeting before they leave the floor** — "demo or dinner?" is the close: book a slot now or claim a dinner seat.

**Swag that works vs vanity swag:**

| Works | Vanity |
|-------|--------|
| Useful daily-carry (good socks, real notebook, charging cable) | Branded stress balls, pens, plastic |
| Earned, not handed (finish the demo → get the good thing) | Bowl of giveaways anyone grabs |
| On-brand and genuinely wanted | Logo'd landfill |
| Drives a conversation or a scan | Drives nothing |

**Speaking-slot-to-pipeline:** a talk is a top-of-funnel ad you don't pay for. Close with a specific, low-friction next step ("text this number for the framework"), capture the opt-ins, and route them like any other lead. One strong talk reliably beats a mid-tier booth on sourced pipeline.

## Post-event: the follow-up window closes fast

Speed is the whole game. **Follow up the same week or the leads go cold.**

```
WITHIN 24–48 HRS:
  - Personalized note referencing what you actually discussed
    (NOT "great to connect at the booth")
  - Hot leads → calendar link + a reason to meet now

WITHIN 1 WEEK:
  - Route every scan to the right owner in CRM, tagged by tier
  - Send the promised resource (deck, teardown, recording)

WEEKS 2–6 (multi-touch nurture):
  - 3–4 touch sequence: value, proof, soft CTA, breakup
  - Hot leads run the normal sales motion
  - Warm leads enter nurture; cold leads enter the newsletter
```

```
SUBJECT: That {topic} problem you mentioned at {Event}

{First} — you said {specific thing they said} over dinner.
Here's the {teardown / framework / number} I promised.

If it's useful, I'll show you how {customer like them} fixed it
in {timeframe}. 20 minutes next week?

{Calendar link}
```

**The long-cycle attribution problem.** Enterprise deals close months after the event, so last-touch attribution will tell you events did nothing. Fix it two ways:

- **Pipeline-sourced** — opportunity created within 30 days of an event touch.
- **Pipeline-influenced** — any open opp where a contact had an event touch in the cycle.

Report both. Hold an event to a **sourced + influenced pipeline** target, not to same-quarter closed revenue. Tie event leads back through `playbooks/demand-generation.md` so they land in one pipeline view.

# Part 2 — B2C Experiential

## Activation types

| Play | What it is | Primary output |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| **Pop-up** | Temporary branded retail/space | Footfall, trial, local PR, content |
| **Sampling / seeding** | Product in hands IRL | Trial → first purchase |
| **Sponsorship activation** | Branded presence at a host event | Reach, association, content |
| **Festival / retail activation** | Booth/zone at a high-traffic venue | Volume sampling, UGC |
| **Guerrilla / stunt** | Unexpected public moment | Earned media, virality |
| **Built-for-the-feed experience** | A space/moment designed to be filmed | UGC volume, social reach |
| **Street teams** | Brand reps in target neighborhoods | Local density, sampling, flyering |

## The content flywheel: 1 event → 100 pieces of content

For B2C, **the room is the smaller audience; the feed is the bigger one.** Design every activation so the people who weren't there see it. The 200 people who attend matter less than the 200,000 who watch the clips.

```
DESIGN THE MOMENT FOR CAPTURE:
  - One "framed" hero moment (a wall, a mirror, a reveal, a giant object)
  - Vertical-friendly: shootable in 9:16 without the logo getting cut
  - A reason to film (surprise, scale, beauty, humor)
  - A natural hashtag + handle, visible but not plastered everywhere

MULTIPLY:
  - Brand films the event (1 shooter = 30–50 clips)
  - Attendees film themselves (UGC = free + more trusted)
  - Partner with 3–8 local creators to attend and post
    (see playbooks/influencer-marketing.md)
  - Cut the footage into Reels/TikToks/Shorts for weeks after

DISTRIBUTE:
  - Owned social, paid amplification of the best UGC,
    creator posts, PR pickup of the stunt
```

**Partner with local creators, not just mega-influencers.** A handful of trusted local voices who actually show up produces more believable content than one celebrity drop-in. Brief them, disclose the partnership (see Compliance), and let them shoot in their own voice. Plug this into `playbooks/influencer-marketing.md` and the broader `playbooks/b2c-distribution-playbook.md`.

## B2C measurement

Pick the metric that matches the goal — don't measure a stunt by footfall.

| Goal | Primary metric | How to capture |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Trial | Samples → trial rate | Unique redemption code per sample |
| Conversion | Code redemption / sales lift | Activation-specific promo code or landing URL |
| Reach | Footfall + impressions | Door counter, venue estimates |
| Social proof | UGC volume + hashtag mentions | Social listening on the campaign tag |
| Earned media | Press pickups + reach | Media monitoring |
| Word-of-mouth | Branded search lift / direct traffic | Pre/post search and traffic by region |

**Always make redemption trackable.** A sample with a unique code tells you trial→purchase; an untracked sample tells you nothing. Same logic as a UTM — see `playbooks/b2c-distribution-playbook.md`.

# Part 3 — Shared: budgeting, runbooks, compliance

## Budgeting and ROI math

Run the break-even before you book. For B2B, the question is **cost per qualified meeting and the deals it must produce.**

```
B2B BREAK-EVEN:
  Total event cost          = E   (sponsorship + travel + staff time + swag)
  Qualified meetings        = M
  Cost per qualified meeting = E / M
  Opps from meetings         = M × win-stage rate
  Deals needed to break even = E / (ACV × gross margin)

  WORKED EXAMPLE (directional):
    E = $8,000 dinner-led event
    M = 12 qualified conversations
    Cost per qualified meeting ≈ $670
    ACV = $20,000 → ONE deal returns 2.5x the event cost
```

**Directional 2026 benchmark ranges** (use as planning anchors, validate with your own data):

| Metric | Weak | Solid | Strong |
|--------|------|-------|--------|
| B2B cost per qualified meeting | > $1,500 | $400–800 | < $400 |
| B2B cost per qualified lead (booth) | > $500 | $150–350 | < $150 |
| B2C cost per sample-to-trial | > $25 | $8–18 | < $8 |
| B2C cost per 1K social impressions (earned) | > $20 | $3–10 | < $3 |
| Dinner → qualified follow-up rate | < 30% | 40–60% | > 60% |

**Events are NOT worth it when:** the motion is pure self-serve with no human sale, your ICP isn't dense at any event, you can't staff fast same-week follow-up, or you'd book a booth purely for fear of missing out. When in doubt below $5M ARR, run the dinner variant first — it's the cheapest way to test whether IRL works for you.

## Cold-start runbook (your first event)

### Variant A — B2B first dinner

```
T-5 WEEKS:  Pick a conference your ICP already attends. Don't sponsor it.
T-4 WEEKS:  Book a private restaurant room near the venue.
            Draft the peer-problem theme (no product in the title).
T-4 to -2:  Invite 25–30 tier A/B contacts. Over-invite 2.5x.
            Recruit a customer or respected guest to anchor the table.
T-1 WEEK:   Confirm headcount. Reconfirm 48h out (kills no-shows).
            Prep one opening question + a moderator.
NIGHT OF:   No pitch. Capture what each guest cares about.
T+48 HRS:   Personalized follow-up referencing their actual words.
T+2 WEEKS:  Route to pipeline; book demos with the warm seats.
SUCCESS:    Full table + 5–8 qualified follow-ups + 1–2 opps.
```

### Variant B — B2C first pop-up

```
T-6 WEEKS:  Pick a high-foot-traffic spot where your customer already is.
            Secure permit + liability insurance (see Compliance).
T-5 WEEKS:  Design ONE hero, feed-ready moment. Make it shootable in 9:16.
T-4 WEEKS:  Line up 3–8 local creators. Brief them; agree disclosure.
T-3 WEEKS:  Tease on owned social. Print unique-code samples.
T-1 WEEK:   Confirm staff/street team, signage, the capture plan, a shooter.
DAY OF:     Run the moment. Film everything. Capture consented contacts.
T+1 WEEK:   Cut 30–50 clips. Amplify the best UGC. Track code redemption.
SUCCESS:    Footfall hit + UGC volume + measurable sample-to-trial rate.
```

## Scale runbook (repeatable playbook)

Turn a winning one-off into a system:

1. **Templatize.** Lock the dinner format / pop-up moment into a repeatable kit (invite copy, run-of-show, packing list, follow-up sequence).
2. **Build the calendar.** Map your category's events 2–3 quarters out; pick the anchor shows and the cities for a roadshow series.
3. **Standardize capture.** Same CRM tags, same scan-tiering, same code structure every time — so events compare cleanly.
4. **Sequence the roadshow.** Repeat the format across 3–5 cities per quarter; reuse 80% of assets each stop.
5. **Graduate to owned.** Past $3M ARR, your own recurring dinner series or user conference compounds retention and expansion — that's where IRL becomes a moat, not a campaign.
6. **Feed the loop.** Log cost-per-meeting and sourced/influenced pipeline per event so the next budget is data-led, not vibes-led.

Run-of-show template for any hosted IRL event:

```
T-60 min:  Staff arrive, AV + signage check, name list at door
T-30 min:  Capture station live (consented opt-in), badges/codes ready
T-0:       Doors. Greeters direct guests; no dead air.
+15 min:   Opening / host frame (the peer problem, NOT the pitch)
+20–80:    Core moment — dinner discussion / activation / demo loop
+80 min:   Soft CTA + the one next step (book / claim / redeem)
+90 min:   Close. Collect contacts. Note hot leads while fresh.
T+0–48h:   Same-week follow-up fires.
```

## Compliance (non-negotiable)

Gate every IRL program against these before it runs:

- **Data-capture consent.** A scanned badge or a sign-up is personal data. Collect with clear opt-in for marketing follow-up (GDPR lawful basis; CAN-SPAM for the emails that follow). Don't import a scraped attendee list and cold-blast it as if they opted in. Log consent source per contact.
- **Permits and insurance.** Pop-ups, street teams, and public activations need venue permits and liability/event insurance. Confirm both in writing before promoting the date.
- **No deceptive activations.** No fake "spontaneous" crowds presented as organic, no staged strangers passed off as real customers, no astroturfed buzz. State who's behind the moment.
- **Disclose paid creators.** Any creator or influencer you pay or gift to attend and post must disclose the relationship (FTC #ad / paid partnership). Put it in the brief; it's their obligation and your liability. See `playbooks/influencer-marketing.md`.
- **Truthful claims on-site.** Demos, signage, and rep scripts carry the same substantiation bar as any ad. No on-floor claim you couldn't put in writing.
